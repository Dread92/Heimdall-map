How Heimdall works

An unofficial air-threat map for Kyiv and the oblasts around it. One Python process reads the official alert data and the channels that track drones and missiles, stores every post before it reads it, rebuilds the map on every pass, and serves the pages and the pushes. This manual is the repository's own documentation, as of version 1.57.1, 11 October 2026: the map first, then everything in full.

Three journeys

The same machinery, followed through one post, one alert and one bulletin.

A post becomes a mark

§7 · geo.py, State.markers()

  1. Read. The previews every 30 s (10 s in a missile threat); three channels through the Telegram API every 15 s. §7.2
  2. Stored first. Into feed before anything reads it, so a fix to the rules re-reads the old posts too.
  3. Parsed. geo.parse_for_channel: weapon, place, count, and a course, height or speed only when stated. §7.3
  4. Placed in context. The channel's report before and after picks between places that share a name; "Вибух" or "500" alone goes to its last report. §7.3b
  5. Chained. A report continues a track only at a speed the target can fly: 350 km/h, a jet drone 650. §7.4
  6. Aged. Grey once no new report comes, then gone; Kyiv's official all-clear ends every position in the city.
  7. Tagged. #n, Heimdall's number for the target tonight. §7.8
  8. Logged, served, pushed. Once into marker_log; out by /api/markers and the live line; a push to the phones whose cell it nears. §9

An alert becomes a colour

§6 · UkraineAlarm, AlertsInUa, Ubilling

  1. Three feeds. The raion feed (siren.pp.ua or the keyed ukrainealarm API), alerts.in.ua with a token, and the ubilling mirror while the raion feed is down. §6.1
  2. Normalised. One shape per region and alert type; start and end events kept for the replay. §6.3
  3. Reconciled. One source decides by precedence; a source that stops answering stops deciding. §6.5
  4. Coloured. Red, yellow or nothing, raion by raion. No Telegram post can start or end it. §6.6
  5. Shown and sent. The pages paint the raions; the start and the end are pushed to the phones that asked.

A window becomes a bulletin

§7.11 · Bulletins, AIReader

  1. On the clock. The last hour every 20 minutes, the last 24 hours every hour, only while something happened. §7.11
  2. The record. activity_summary: counts, places, times, heights, courses, missiles, weather. Never a post's text, a channel or a reader.
  3. One call. OpenRouter writes it in Ukrainian, English and French, within the month's budget.
  4. Cleaned. No markdown, links, channel names or card numbers; cut at a full sentence.
  5. Kept. Every one for 90 days with its record, then one an hour and one a day for good.
  6. Shown. In the app only with the dashboard's switch on, and only while the server still stands by it.

The ground rules

Each is pinned by tests and argued in the safety rules.

  • COLOUR
    Only the official data sets an alert's colour, raion by raion. Telegram cannot start or end an alert.
  • FACTS
    Never a trajectory, ETA, threat type, altitude or position a post did not state.
  • IMMEDIATE
    Ballistic, Kh-22/32 and MiG-31K concern the whole region: no radius, no countdown.
  • STALE
    A target with no new report turns grey, then goes. Stale is never shown as live.
  • NEWS
    A press release, a tally or reported speech is not an observation of the sky.
  • BANDEROL
    A jet drone, never a cruise missile: in parsing, tags, chaining and every language.
  • PLACES
    Home, Work, Kids and Pin never leave the phone; a push subscription keeps a ~10 km cell.
  • UNSTATED
    A weapon nobody named is "probably a drone", or "drone or missile" while missiles are reported.
  • TRACE
    Every mark leads back to its post, its channel, its time and the rule that read it.
  • SECRETS
    Keys are the owner's Fly secrets; config, VAPID keys and databases are never committed or printed.

1. What it is#

An unofficial air-raid and drone tracker for Kyiv city and Kyiv oblast first, and the oblasts switched on around them (the six around Kyiv, then Odesa, Kharkiv, Dnipro, Mykolaiv and, since 1.45, seven more — §7.1). One Python process — standard library only, plus cryptography for Web Push — reads:

  • the official «Повітряна тривога» alert data (which raions are under alert, at which level),
  • 23 public Telegram channels that report where drones and missiles are: the three Kyiv live trackers, three country-wide channels (the Air Force among them) and one live tracker per oblast (§7.1) — most through their t.me/s/ previews, three through the Telegram API,
  • the Air Force's summaries and the carriers' take-offs and launches, read from those posts (§7.5, §7.6), and the nights' weather (Open-Meteo, §7.6),

and serves its pages from the same data — and, with the owner's key, has an AI model write a bulletin of the last hour and the last 24 hours from what it recorded (§7.11):

Page Address For
Tactical /m (also /, /k, /kyiv) The full map: every mark with its evidence, the feed, alerts, statistics, display modes, layers.
Light /light (also /l) One place (one of the four pins, named by their owner): its raion's official status, what is within 30 km, what is heading there.
Guide /guide What to do, weapon by weapon and situation by situation — the official guidance, in three languages, offline (1.35, §10.5).

Both install as separate PWAs (manifest ids /m and /light — unchanged by the 1.29 rename, so Android offers the new name and icon as an update of the installed app; an iPhone keeps an installed app's name and icon until it is added again). Production runs on Fly.io as kyiv-air-watch-gb (one performance-1x machine — a dedicated CPU — with 2 GB since 1.41.1, a 1 GB volume for the database; [[vm]] in fly.toml, which every deploy applies).

2. Principles the code enforces#

Summarised here, argued in SAFETY.md, and each pinned by tests:

  • The official alert decides the colour. Red / yellow / nothing comes only from the official data, by raion. A Telegram post can neither start nor end an alert (OFFICIAL_ALERTS_FROM_TELEGRAM = False).
  • Nothing is invented. No trajectory, ETA, threat type, altitude or position the post did not state. A course is drawn only if the post reported it; an ETA is a band, only toward the watched place, only for a stated course, never for ballistic / Kh-22 / MiG-31K.
  • Weapons that outrun a radius ignore the radius. Ballistic, supersonic (Kh-22/32) and MiG-31K are "immediate": they concern the whole region (150 km), with no distance and no countdown.
  • Stale is never shown as live. A target with no new report turns grey, then disappears.
  • A news item is not an observation of the sky. Press releases, tallies and reported speech never become marks.
  • Banderol (S-8000) is a jet drone, never a cruise missile — in parsing, tags, chaining and every language.
  • Places stay on the phone. The four watched places are never sent to the server; a push subscription stores a ~10 km cell, never a village; the Light page works out the oblast and raion on the phone.
  • Every mark is traceable to the post, the channel, the time and the rule that read it.

5.1 The process#

python app/server.py (--demo for fake alerts, --port N). One ThreadingHTTPServer and these threads:

Thread What it does Cadence
UkraineAlarm official alerts by raion (keyless proxy or keyed API) /alerts/status every 10 s, full list on change or every 30 s
AlertsInUa official alerts from alerts.in.ua, when a token is set 15 s
Ubilling oblast-level mirror; applies only while the raion source is down 30 s
Telegram reads the channels' t.me/s/ previews (all but those the API reads) 30 s; 10 s while a missile threat is open or a threat is within 12 km of a Kyiv bridge (1.34)
TelegramAPI reads telegram_api_channels (chyste_nebo) through the Telegram client API — polls the channel, never subscribes to updates last 20 posts every 15 s (8 s in a missile threat)
Vitals 1.41: the process's own CPU, the CPU the host took away (steal — on a shared Fly vCPU, throttling), memory, how late its own clock woke it; every request over 2 s (path and time only); every start with its build (kv.boots, last 100). On the dashboard's Live tab (/api/online → process) 10 s
Watchdog the database and alert-state locks must be free within 15 s; a held lock logs every thread's stack, five in a row (~2 min) → exit, Fly restarts it; a starved process is only logged (load, not a deadlock) 20 s
Digests each night (18:00–08:00 Kyiv) after digest_hour, and each Monday for the week: the figures (State.digest_facts), and with ANTHROPIC_API_KEY a written summary in EN + UK; stored in digests 5 min check
Translations machine-translates feed posts into a language somebody reads on demand, ~3 calls/s max
bulletins 1.54, with OPENROUTER_API_KEY: the AI's bulletins (§7.11) — on the clock, the last hour and the last 24 hours once an hour at :00 (1.56.1; the last hour was every 20 minutes), only while there is activity, one call each in three languages → ai_bulletins; or one the dashboard asks for. (Until 1.53 an ai-reader thread read the posts — §7.7; retired) 20 s; ≤ ai_reader_monthly_usd a month
night-reports 1.53: after ai_report_hour (Kyiv), the AI's report on the night before (§7.10) → ai_reports; or a night the dashboard asks for 5 min
Pusher sends Web Push: one notification at a time, each to all its phones at once through push.Sender (32 workers, one kept-open HTTPS connection per push service per worker, VAPID signed once an hour per service) queue
streams every open page's live line (StreamHub, §5.4): one thread, non-blocking sockets (epoll), the data sent as changes events + every 15 / 5 / 2 s
proximity per-subscriber distance checks → push continuous
AlertsInUa.backfill a month of history for the favourite regions once at start
AFBackfill the Air Force summaries of the last 14 days that af_reports lacks: first from the stored feed, then from t.me/s/kpszsu?before=… pages (at most 240, stops once nothing is missing); since 1.33 also the carriers (parse_air_report) in every stored post, once per parser version (kv.air_parsed) 60 s after start, then daily
bridges every 5 s: a Kyiv bridge whose state rose (nothing → near → at, §9b) is written to bridge_events and pushed to the phones that asked for the bridges (topic bridges, in their language), at most once per bridge every 10 min — a "near" turning "at" excepted 5 s

One computation for everybody (State.cached): /api/markers, /api/state, /api/feed, /api/stats, /api/impacts are built once per change (the publish sequence seq) and at most every few seconds, by one thread while the others wait, serialised and gzipped once; every page gets the same bytes (_send_cached). The proximity pushes use the same marks (markers_now()). Nothing in a request path calls the network: the marks' English text is the offline glossary (machine translation is the Translations worker's alone). The listen backlog is 128. Measured on a 200-post window: ~1,000 requests/s, p95 ≈ 50 ms, where 1.23 managed 3/s.

poll_gap() shortens the official-source and Telegram intervals while a missile threat is open. The live line beats faster with it (msl 0 → every 15 s, 1 → 5 s, 2 → 2 s); a page whose line is down falls back to asking /api/version at 15 / 5 / 1 s, as before 1.29.

At start the process raises its open-file limit to what the system allows (up to 65,536): every open page is a socket, and Linux's default of 1,024 would stop the server long before the hub does.

5.4 The live line (1.29)#

Before 1.29 each open page held one server thread in /api/stream, and every new post made every page download the marks, the feed and the alerts again — five requests per page per post. On the 256 MB machine of the time Linux allowed ~2,000 threads, so ~1,700 open pages was a hard stop, and the CPU cost grew with pages × posts.

Now Handler._stream sends the headers and hands the socket to StreamHub (the connection is not closed: Server.shutdown_request skips it). The hub writes to every page from one thread, each message serialised once. What it sends:

  • the old events, unchanged, for a page that asked for them (ev=1, the default): start, end, threat, update, feed, eradar, tr, notice — the Tactical page raises its toasts and sounds from them;
  • hello on connecting: {v, msl, hb, now, h: {part: hash}} — which version of each part is current;
  • sync after a burst of events (150 ms to gather it) and at least every hb seconds (15 / 5 / 2 by missile level — the heartbeat): {v, msl, hb, now} plus, for each part that changed and that the page asked for (sync=mk,st,fd), what changed:
    • mk (the marks, by id) and fd (the feed's 150 posts, by post_id): set = changed entries, same order; pre + n = new entries at the head (a new post, a new mark); ids = a new order; all = the whole list when there is nothing to diff against;
    • st (the alerts state): act = the active alerts as a list (by location_uid|alert_type), sub / unset = keys of oblasts and sources that changed, set = other values whole. (1.46: sources is {name: {ok}} — the time of each reading changed at every poll and was resent to every page at every beat; the detail is the dashboard's, /api/health.) Every part carries b (the version it starts from) and h (the version it leads to). The full answers (/api/markers, /api/state, /api/feed) carry the same h. A page applies a change only when it holds exactly b (static/live.js, LiveSync.part), otherwise it fetches that part in full — nothing is guessed. tests/test_live.py checks that the deltas rebuild exactly what the server serves.

The pages: while the line beats (a message within 3 × hb), they ask nothing; the Tactical page checks /api/version once a minute (build, safety) and does a full reload every 10 minutes. A silent line is reopened, and the page asks at its old cadence until it beats again. The Light page takes sync=mk,st without events (p=light&ev=0); the Tactical page in blackout mode takes mk,st and still fetches its 25-post feed.

A page that stops reading is dropped once 512 KB are waiting for it (its EventSource reconnects). Nothing about a reader is kept: a connection is a socket, a language and which page it is; /api/online counts them.

Measured on the demo feed (one core, 2026-09-28): 9,000 open pages — 8 threads, 62 MB, ~80 ms of CPU per second, a change reaching the last page ~0.1 s after the first; ~25 KB per page per minute under the demo's heavy churn. The same pages on 1.28 needed 9,000 threads and ~45,000 requests per post.

5.5 What the process spends its CPU on (1.46)#

Measured on 7 Oct 2026 with py-spy on a copy of the service, the channels read at the ballistic cadence (every 5 s), the feed of that evening loaded: 87 % of the CPU went into building TLS contexts. urllib.request.urlopen() without a context makes a new one per request and loads the system's ~150 root certificates into it — 15–25 ms of CPU each time, for each of the ~20 channel previews and the alert sources, every round. One shared context (server._SSL_CTX, translate._CTX, passed to every urlopen) took the process from 10 % of a core to 1.6 %; with 500 pages on the live line and a full page load every second, 4.1 % and 113 MB of memory. This is very likely what spent a shared vCPU's burst balance on attack nights (§15, "throttling") before 1.41.1 moved to a dedicated CPU.

Also in 1.46: the pages' state carries whether each source works and nothing more (§5.4); the oblasts a post names are read once per post (State._named_cache, they were ~40 % of each rebuild of the marks); /api/places is built once an hour per set of oblasts and sent gzipped (State.cached), and the Tactical page asks for oblast=all only when the villages layer is turned on (loadVillages); SQLite runs synchronous=NORMAL (with WAL: a sync at each checkpoint, not at each commit).

Not changed, already economical: the live line (one thread, one serialisation per combination of parts, deltas), the 304s on static files, Web Push's pooled connections.

5.6 Zooming the map (1.49)#

The map is one SVG in kilometres; the view is its viewBox. Measured in Chromium with the CPU slowed 4× (a mid-range phone), a pinch of 200 frames: every change of the viewBox restyled every element sized by --k and laid out every label again — 50 ms a frame (20 fps), the main thread busy 100 % of the time (style 49 %, layout 28 %). A change of scale lays the SVG's text out again even when it is only a CSS transform of the SVG or of an element around it. So, while a pinch or the wheel goes on (zoomAt(…, true) → fastVB), the map drawn last is moved and scaled on the screen with a CSS transform (svg#map, will-change:transform, from its top-left corner), the names of places are hidden for that moment (svg.zoomg :not(.mk)>text; the marks and their labels stay), and 150 ms after the last movement the new view is drawn once (applyVB()). The view's arithmetic uses the map's box without the transform (SR()). Result: 16.7 ms a frame (60 fps) on the slowed phone and on a computer, the main thread busy 20 %; the redraw at rest costs one frame of ~70 ms. A one-finger drag still moves the viewBox (no change of scale: cheap).

1.54.3, the owner: "sometimes the map flickers when I zoom in or out" — black patches over the map, the left column blank. The scaled map is one GPU picture only while nothing inside it moves; every mark's halo, a blinking glyph, a marching course ray made the browser draw the whole map again at every frame, at the scaled size — and a long wheel zoom scaled it to many times the screen, more than the GPU keeps. Now, while the gesture lasts, every animation inside the map is paused (svg.zoomg *{animation-play-state:paused}), and a scale past 3× or below ⅓ is drawn again at its own size at once (fastVB → applyVB()), so the scaled picture never grows past three screens. The glow of an oblast with a target somewhere in it no longer breathes (a large clipped area repainted 60 times a second for nothing a reader needs).

Tiles: the tiles of the zoom before stay under the new ones until all of these have loaded or failed (tileSweep, 4 s at most), so a change of zoom never shows the black background between two pictures.

Zoomed in, nothing large is dashed or animated (svg.deep, past ~90 px a km). Chrome draws a dashed line dash by dash along its whole length — the 50 km ring around Kyiv is 25,000 dashes at 1 km across — and an animated one again at every frame: 1.48 could stall a computer's page ("page not responding") at the closest zooms. There the rings, the zones, the occupied areas' outline, the region rings, the reach and course rays and the tracks are drawn solid and still, the occupied areas in a plain tint.

Lengths sized by the zoom keep their digits (kx(), KDIG): with two decimals, at 1 km across a drone's scale(0.0007) was written scale(0.00) and the mark vanished; marks are placed to the metre.

6.5 Reconciliation#

7.2 From page to stored post#

Two readers, one path. Telegram.poll() parses a channel's preview HTML; TelegramAPI re-reads a channel's last 20 posts every 15 s (8 s in a missile threat), with receive_updates=False: a personal account's update stream (every chat it is in) took the 256 MB server down within minutes on 25 Sep 2026. ingest() runs in a worker thread (asyncio.to_thread), off the client's event loop. Both hand (post_id, time, text) to Telegram.ingest(), so a post read through the API is stored, tagged, parsed and shown exactly like one read from a preview. The preview reader skips a channel the API is reading (api_channels).

Telegram.ingest() skips posts already stored, runs tag_feed_text() and is_relevant() (news, fundraising, culture → dropped), stores the post with the offline English (§8) and publishes a feed event. Tagging order matters: forecasts first (FORECAST_RX — "оцінка загроз", "може відбутись у будь-який момент", and since 1.41.2 what the enemy "plans": "ворог планує запустити …" — never raise anything), then the news guard (geo.looks_like_news: strong news words, reported speech and consequences, length, summaries, and since 1.41.2 looks_like_explainer — a live tracker talking about the sky: "до прикладу", "розповім про балістику", "по цій темі", "я описую ціль", "якщо ціль знищена …", "ворог планує"), then threat tags. parse_for_channel applies looks_like_explainer to every channel's parser, so none of it is ever a mark. 1.42.1: a retraction comes before all of it (is_retraction()): a glitch of the system ("лаг системи", "збій системи", "хибне спрацювання") on its own, or "not confirmed" / a false alert about something missile-shaped (a speed, a launch, a rocket, a MiG) — "Хибна ціль" is not one, that is a decoy, which flies. It is tagged retract and nothing else, kept (is_relevant), and it ends what the channels said before it: a ballistic or supersonic mark of the 20 minutes before (_drop_retracted(), also in the replay), the missile mode (_missile_active() stops at it), and the warning strip's channel-raised ballistic / MiG-31K warnings. Never an official one — only the official data ends an alert. "… не підтвердився" about anything else is an explainer: no mark. banderol_missiles removes cruise_missiles and unspecified_missiles.

7.3 From post to mark (geo.py)#

parse_for_channel(channel, text) picks the channel's parser; each returns candidates with an evidence block for every value:

  • Type — TYPE_RX: drones (Shahed / Geran / "мопед"), jet drones, cruise, ballistic, supersonic (Kh-22/32, before cruise), banderol_missiles (Banderol / S-8000 / "реактивні ракети з БпЛА"), KAB, tactical and strategic aviation, MiG-31K. No type word → unknown (drawn as an upright ⚠, never guessed).
  • Position — the gazetteer with declension-aware stems (hyphen lookbehind: Коцюбинське ≠ Михайло-Коцюбинське); aliases like "Велика Димерка" ← "димерк"; a stated part of an oblast is not its centre. 1.56.3: the core gazetteer's coordinates (geo.PLACES, written by hand) checked against OpenStreetMap's settlements (static/streets/): 82 of 379 matched names were more than 1.5 km off and are where OSM puts them now — Гнідин was 5.8 km east of the village (the owner: "Hnidyn is never reported there"), Березань 11 km, Ольшаниця 12, Мигалки 14, Котельва 19; Красилівка and Ясногородка, several of each, are the one near Brovary and the one near Makariv.
  • Heading — "курсом на", compass words, "→", "A - B" routes (bearing()); a heading is marked stated only when the post stated it. A compass word after "з / зі / від" is where it comes from ("з півночі" = flying south), also after the destination (_FROM_COMPASS).
  • Where it is vs where it is going (_place_role): a place after "на", "в район", "до", "в напрямку", "в бік" is the destination; after "від", "з", "повз", "над", "біля", "в районі" it is where the target is. After "на", a place in the locative ("на Позняках", "на Троєщині") is where it is. Only a destination named ("на Васильків з північного заходу", "курсом на Київ"): the mark is drawn at the destination as an approach (approach: true, place: "→ X", low confidence) — also when the post's only other place is its own oblast heading. On live channels, "Від Глевахи на Васильків" is at Hlevakha heading for Vasylkiv; "йдуть на Васильків" alone (dest_only) stays at the channel's previous report (≤ 8 min) turned toward the destination, or else is an approach. In tracking, a later approach about a target already on the map gives it its destination and course instead of moving it; an approach point is never a step of a track.
  • Count, altitude (only when written: "знижується", "низько", metres "висота 2200", a bare "1600, Вороньків" in a short live post, kilometres "висота 4,4км"), phase (PHASE_LADDER: prep → launch → entering…, with a per-sentence hedge guard: "може відбутись" is preparation, not a launch).
  • The oblast, then its towns (1.42.1): "курсом на Київщину, Макарів / Наливайківку" — a town the post names right after the oblast it is in is the destination, not the oblast's centre.
  • The next target (1.41.3, _split_next) — an outcome followed by a sentence starting "наступний", "ще один", "другий" or "інший" ("1 знищено.\nНаступний Нивки") is two reports. The outcome keeps only its own words (on a live channel the previous position, needs_context; otherwise no mark); the place after the word is the next target's position, never an outcome. Read whole, it was a confirmed shoot-down at Nyvky, shown 24 h in the history layer.
  • Immediate types — IMMEDIATE_TYPES = {ballistic, supersonic, mig31k}, REGION_ALERT_KM = 150.

7.3b The regional trackers: their villages, their Russian (1.43)#

Kharkiv city's districts are named by hand in the builder (CURATED_BY_NAME).

  • They are answered by PLACES[name] like any place (_Gazetteer) but never iterated: not in the regex index, not in the map's town labels, not in /api/places. They are matched only in their oblast's context — a post from a channel whose home is that oblast, or a sentence that names the oblast (_find_places(text, ctx_uid) → _find_regional). "Іванівка" alone, from Kyiv, is nothing.
  • Words that are also a village's name are never read as one (REGIONAL_STOP: the compass, the border, "new", 1.43.1 "буде / будет" and the alert levels "жовтий / червоний" …; _REG_PAIR_STOP: a verb is never half of a two-word name — "новий буде" is not Нова Буда; an oblast's adjective "Київська / Черкаський" is never the village "Київське", _ADJ_NOT_NEUTER_RX); REGIONAL_WORDS gives a bare word its oblast's meaning ("Водосховище" from Chernihiv = the Kyiv reservoir).
  • 1.43.1: a core town of the next oblast against hamlets of its name — "На Таращу" from the Cherkasy tracker is the town of Тараща (Kyiv oblast), not the hamlet in Cherkasy oblast: when every village of the channel's oblast with that name is a small place (kind P, < 2,000), the name becomes an ambiguity token with the core town as its default and the villages as the other candidates (so the channel's previous report or the post's other places can still pick the village).
  • A name several villages share becomes one ambiguity token (_ambig_token) with its candidates: the one near another place the same post names (≤ 60 km) wins at once; otherwise the server takes the one nearest the channel's report of the last 30 minutes (≤ 60 km, _marks_from_posts); with neither, the clear default — the only city district, the only raion/hromada centre, or a population three times the next — or no mark at all. A destination nobody can tell apart gets no course drawn to it.
  • Routes: "Любеч-Славутич", "Сновськ-Березна-Куликівка", "Лесное - Пятихатки" — two places joined by a dash are at the first, heading for the second (_route_arrows writes "→"), unless the joined word is one place ("Новгород-Сіверський") or it follows "в районі / між" ("в районі Котельва - Коломак" is an area, not a course).
  • One line, several reports: a count of weapons after the words of the previous report starts a new one ("2 реакти … 1 реакт …"), so does ", 1 …" ("1 Райгород, 1 Буки на Бузівка, 1 на Привітне").
  • Words: "реакт / реакти" (a jet drone, JET_RX), FPV, "Молния", "Ланцет" (small strike drones).
  • 1.44: an oblast named as the origin — "Реактивний БпЛА з Дніпропетровщини на Світлогірське" from the Poltava tracker: after "з / із / від", the oblast is where it came from, and the sentence's villages are read in the channel's own oblast (parse_post, home).   • 1.44.1: a list after "в бік / курс" ("в бік Велика Рибиця/Мала Рибиця/Осоївка", "на Димерку, Бровари") is a list of places it may be heading for: every name joined by /, ,, "або / чи / і" to a destination is a destination (_LIST_SEP_RX in _parse_live) — read as a bare name, the second one became where the target was. 1.56.4, the owner: "Jet UAV to Kyiv/Irpin/Bucha — the post is talking about Bucha, yet the drone is put in the middle of the city": a target heading for a list and nowhere stated is drawn, as an approach, at the one geo.approach_pick chooses — in Kyiv and its oblast not the city when its suburbs are named with it, and of the rest the farthest from the centre (a target heading for all of them reaches the outermost first); elsewhere the first named, as before — and the mark keeps the whole list (targets), which the pages say as written ("heading for Київ / Ірпінь / Буча", apprTo in i18n.js). 7.4 Scope, chaining, staleness#

State.markers():

  1. parses the recent feed into candidates; merges identical ones from the same post (a count). A live-tracking channel's bare line ("На Таращу", "Вибух", "Не спостерігається") is placed at that channel's last reported position (last_by_channel, at most 8 min old). 1.38: that position keeps its own time — the mark carries pos_ts, and a carried position never restarts the clock (only a newly named place does); the channel's own down / lost / clear line ends it; and the official all-clear for Kyiv city ends every position held inside the city (State.kyiv_city_all_clears: an end of the city's air-raid alert that left no other source's alert running). A live tracker's bare place with no course gets the bearing from its previous report (last_by_channel, ≤ 8 min, ≥ 0.4 km) — 1.56.3: only when that post and this one each report one target (n_here, the ctx's 4th field: the previous post's Kiptiv → this post's Liubech drew a target heading NW, back to Belarus — two targets, not a course), and never one that points back across the border (outward, below);
  2. drops what is outside the scope: in_scope() = oblast uid in SCOPE_UIDS (1.43: the oblasts switched on — Kyiv, the six around it, Odesa, Kharkiv, Dnipro, 1.44 Mykolaiv, 1.45 seven more by default) or, with no oblast, within SCOPE_KM = 330 km of Kyiv. REGION_UIDS (Kyiv and the six around it) still decides the feed's "region" tag;
  3. _chain_and_prune() links consecutive reports of the same target (same family, time and distance windows, roughly ahead of the earlier heading) into a track with history — each entry keeps its own report's stated height (alt_m, alt_state), so the pages can show the heights the posts gave in sequence; marks stale after track_stale_minutes; drops an unknown after 5 min, others after max(stale × 3, 15) min — all counted from the position's time (pos_ts, else the post's); drops everything in an oblast with no active alert of any kind once 3 min old, and, in Kyiv city with no alert running, every mark reported before the last official all-clear there; missiles age on their own MISSILE_TTL_MIN; 1.43.1, the chaining windows (_chain): a report continues a track of its own channel within 30 min, of another channel within 15 min; two channels' unspecified threats (bare place names) are one target only at a drone's speed (7 km/min, not 15); a report naming only the oblast (place == "область", drawn at the oblast centre) never takes over a report that named a place, a destination ("на Зміїв") is never handed to one, and its point is never a step of a track's history. Before this, the Kyiv channels' bare names — one a minute — took over every Odesa, Chernihiv and Kharkiv report within 475 km and 30 min, and those oblasts' reports were never drawn. 1.54.4, a drone's reach (the owner: "not 100 % sure, but no wild assumption when the gap between two points is so huge" — #157, "Сквира" 00:29 then "Велика Димерка" 00:53, one track 150 km across Kyiv in 24 minutes): a drone's report continues a track only within min(3.5 km/min × dt + 15, 80) km — a Shahed's 210 km/h with a tailwind — a jet drone's within min(9 × dt + 15, 120); an unstated type is a drone when it is a bare place name of a channel that tracks drones (likely) or another channel's; other unstated types and missiles keep 15 × dt + 25. The tapped target's red dots (§7.8) leave out the line between two reports a drone could not have flown between, by the same measure — the dots stay (drawSelPath, cut). 1.54.7, the physical check (the owner: "if attaching a new report to a target implies 1,000 km/h, reject the match — take the technical spec of the Shahed, the jet Shahed and the Banderol"): reach_km(dt, jet) = VMAX_KMH (350 km/h for a Shahed — 185 by its specification; 650 for a jet drone — the Geran-3 550–600, the Banderol about 500, 650 at most) × the minutes between the two reports + POS_TOL_KM = 8 km (two named places, each a few km from where the target was), never past REACH_CAP_KM (80 / 120 km; a Banderol has no cap). The minutes count from when the earlier report's position was reported (pos_ts: "500" carries a place nine minutes old). A Banderol is a jet drone here too (it had a missile's 15 × dt + 25). The same reach decides between places that share a name (§7.3b) and leaves out a line on the card (cut: d>(jet?650:350)/60*dt+8). 1.43.1, the regional clock: a report from a channel with a regional home (geo.CHANNEL_HOME) is stale after REGIONAL_STALE_MIN = 10 min and dropped after REGIONAL_GONE_MIN = 25 (an unknown after 10) — the trackers post every five to twenty minutes; it carries slow: true, and the map fades it on the same clock (grey at 10, fainter at 15, "contact lost" at 20). The oblast's all-clear still ends it at once;
  4. outcomes (down, impact, lost) close the track they belong to.

What it is for, first: finding what the rules misread or miss, so that the rules are fixed — they stay deterministic, free and instant. What it reads. Telegram.ingest hands it (_to_ai, off the post's path: a queue, read on its own thread) every new post of a channel on the map — never one on trial or off — that the tagger saw a target or a place in (AIReader.WANT), and, from the posts the rules set aside as not about the sky, those that name a place and are 300 characters or shorter (a tracker's line the rules did not understand, "Ціль на Бровари"; a long one is news). Not the Air Force's summaries, not a retraction. The post as the reader sees it (_clean_post).

What is sent (OPENROUTER_URL, one request a post): the post's public text, the channel's name and its oblast, the channel's previous posts of the last 40 minutes (at most 3, Store.ai_previous, context only), and the candidate places — the gazetteer's places the post's words match (geo._find_places, the regional villages of the oblasts in play) and those oblasts, at most 30, each with an id (c1…, o<uid>). Never anything about a reader, a watched place, a device or an address; a run of 13–19 digits (a card number in a fundraising line) is replaced before anything is sent or stored (mask).

The prompt is a component, versioned (PROMPT_VERSION, stored with every reading; 3 since 1.51, a p after it when the answer was asked for by the prompt alone). 1.51: it is the second reader of a deterministic parser and is told so — its job is to cross-check the rules' reading against the post, the channel's previous posts and the targets on the map from every channel, to correct it, and above all to add the targets the rules missed ("a target missed is worse than a duplicate"), never inventing: every fact still quotes the post and every place is a candidate. Each post comes with (user_message): the channel and the oblast it speaks for and the oblasts its last posts named (channel_block — a regional tracker's bare name is read in those oblasts); the PARSER READING — the rules' marks for this very post, one line each (parser_lines); and ON THE MAP NOW — the tagged targets other posts placed in the candidates' oblasts or within 80 km of them, newest first, at most 10 (State.ai_context, never the post's own marks). Its answer can say which of those a target is (same_as, a tag it was shown — anything else is dropped) and a vertical state (vertical: descending, climbing, low, high, quoted).

  1. fixed rules from SAFETY.md (SYSTEM): never infer; every fact quotes the post; places only from the candidates, by id, never coordinates; "на X" is heading to X, not at X; a city named with no place in it is the city; the weapon words (Banderol a jet drone, never a cruise missile); the status words; previous posts are context, never a source of facts; an alert or an all-clear alone is no target; 1.50.2: the origin — a direction or a country or area ("з півночі", "з Білорусі", "з Брянщини", "з моря", "з Криму", "з ТОТ"), never a Ukrainian oblast or town, which is a place it comes from — and the speed; a height or a speed belongs to the target of its own line or paragraph, a height on a line of its own to the target just above it;
  2. worked examples (EXAMPLES, six, as earlier turns of the conversation);
  3. the post's context, assembled per post (user_message);
  4. a strict JSON schema (SCHEMA: targets with kind, count, status, place_id, relation, side, distance_km, origin, heading, altitude_m, speed_kmh, each fact with its *_quote; uncertain) — response_format: json_schema, strict, and provider.require_parameters so that no provider ignores it. Nothing in it is nullable (1.50.2): what a post does not state is "", 0 or "none" on the wire (wire, unwire; the examples are written with None and sent in that form). A nullable field is a "union type" to Anthropic's structured outputs, which take at most 16 in a schema, and 1.50.0's 15 came back as "HTTP 400: Provider returned error" on every post. A provider that still refuses the schema (400) is asked once more by the prompt and the examples alone, without the schema and the caching marks (plain); if that works it is kept (AIReader.way, format on the dashboard: schema / prompt), an answer wrapped in a code fence is still read (_json_of); refused both ways, it pauses 10 minutes. A refusal shows the provider's own words (error.metadata.raw, _why), not only OpenRouter's. The request carries no other model parameter (1.50.1): with require_parameters, one the model does not take — the temperature claude-haiku-5.5 does not — leaves no provider at all (OpenRouter's "HTTP 404: No endpoints found that can handle the requested parameters"). A model set in ai_reader_model must take response_format.

The fixed part comes first and the post last, so a provider can reuse the unchanged beginning (prompt caching; for Anthropic's models its end is marked cache_control).

Checked against the post (validate): a quote that is not in the post drops its fact; a place id that was not offered drops the place; a count needs a number in its words, a distance must be written, an altitude must be 30–20,000 m with a figure in its quote, a speed 30–5,000 km/h with a figure and "км" in its quote; an origin's quote must hold a word of its class (ORIGIN_WORDS: "білорус", "брянщ", "мор", "крим", "окупов"…, or a compass word for a direction) — "з Чернігівщини" is never an origin; a target with nothing left (no weapon, no place, no outcome) is dropped. The number of facts dropped is stored (dropped) — a model that keeps inventing shows here.

Compared with the rules (compare, on geo.parse_for_channel of the same text): agree; place (the places differ); kind (another weapon at the same place — a jet drone is the rules' drone or Banderol); status (shot down / impact); ai_only (only the model found a target — perhaps a miss of the rules); rules_only; none; error. A side of a town ("на півночі від Миколаєва") matches the rules' oblast for it (_rel_settle), and Kyiv named alone is the city to both (_city_settle).

Cost and limits. The month's spend is OpenRouter's own figure for each call (usage.cost), kept in kv ai_usd:YYYY-MM; at ai_reader_monthly_usd (10) it stops until the 1st; at most 400 posts an hour; a refused key, no credit, or a model or parameter no provider serves (401 / 402 / 403 / 404) pauses it 10 minutes, "too many requests" (429) one minute; a post that arrives meanwhile is not read (counted in skipped). With the default model (anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5, $0.10 / $0.50 per million tokens) a post costs ~$0.0003–0.0005: the $10 cap is ~25,000 posts a month.

The dashboard (GET /api/ai_reads, 🔒): the state (key, model, prompt version, paused, last error), the month's readings by verdict, the spend against the cap, the median answer time, and the readings — disagreements by default, all on request — each with the post, the rules' places and the model's targets with their quotes. /api/health carries the same state (ai_reader). Readings are kept 90 days.

The switches (1.51, POST /api/admin/ai 🔒, {reader: bool, live: bool}, on the AI reader tab): reader (kv ai_reader_on) stops every call at once — AIReader.enabled() reads it; live (kv ai_live) is the kill switch for its facts in the target tags — State.ai_live(); the marks are recomputed at the next ask (≤ 10 s). 9. Notifications#

  • Web Push (push.py; the VAPID key pair is generated on first run and kept in the kv table). A subscription stores the browser's coarse location snapped to a ~10 km cell (HOME_CELL_KM), never a name.
  • How it is sent (1.29): all the phones at once — push.Sender, 32 sends in flight, each worker keeping one open HTTPS connection per push service, the VAPID signature made once an hour per service. It used to be one phone at a time on a new connection (~0.15 s per Android phone, up to ~0.5 s per iPhone): with a thousand subscribers the last one heard of a ballistic missile minutes late. tests/test_push_fanout.py: 400 phones against a 50 ms push service in well under 3 s, over at most 32 connections.
  • What is pushed: MiG-31K airborne (country-wide), ballistic threat (Kyiv city/oblast), and from proximity_watch: a live, non-stale target within the subscriber's radius (+ half a cell), throttled to one push per 2 min per device — except immediate types, which alert the whole region with no throttle. Siren-start and all-clear pushes were removed on purpose (nothing to act on, and they train people to swipe).
  • In the page: the Tactical page raises toasts with sound/vibration scaled by zone (near / approach / observe). The sound has a volume (the bell: slider 0–100 %, 0 = off, 50 % for a new reader, kept in localStorage as sndvol): every tone goes through one Web Audio master gain, squared from the slider (50 % = −12 dB), because an iPhone ignores an <audio> element's volume. 100 % is the pre-1.28 level; letting go of the slider or ▶ (test) plays the loudest alert at the chosen level. The zones keep their relative levels under it. Push notifications (app closed) use the phone's own notification sound and volume.
  • The escalation level (1.36, escLevel()), 0–4, from what is already known — never from a guess: 1 an official alert over the watched place's own raion (or, with no place set, over Kyiv); 2 a live target within 30 km of the place, or one a post says is heading for it; 3 within 10 km, or a target at a Kyiv bridge (with the bridges on); 4 a fresh ballistic or MiG-31K warning, or an immediate type over the region. It colours the card's edge (#mycard.lv2–lv4) and the bell's dot, and picks the tone: l1 one soft sine (an official alert starting), l2 two short beeps, l3 three rising beeps twice, l4 a 3-second two-tone warble with no gap — each with its vibration (VIB_L). At most one tone in 10 s unless the level rises (TONE), so a busy night does not become a noise people mute. The bell's ▶ plays l2, l3, l4 in turn at the chosen volume. The satellite layer (1.47). Layers → "Satellite imagery (Sentinel-2, 2021)", under everything, inside Ukraine only (#uaclip), at every zoom, on by default on every device (1.47.1; it was off on phones). It and the OSM streets are one choice or the other (1.47.1): checking one unchecks the other — under the satellite, zooming in fell back to the streets every time. 10.3 The switch#

Both pages carry the LIGHT | TACTICAL switch (UK: ПРОСТА | ТАКТИЧНА, FR: LÉGÈRE | TACTIQUE): a full-width two-segment control on Light, each half captioned; a high-contrast pill in the Tactical header.

1.40: a toggle, not a reload. The orange half is one thumb (::before); a tap on the other half (a[data-sw], handled in static/i18n.js, swGo()) slides it across in 0.2 s, then opens the other page. Three things keep the screen from going blank in between:

  • cross-document view transitions (@view-transition{navigation:auto} in Tactical, Light and the guide): the browser keeps the old page on screen until the new one has painted, then fades one into the other (0.16 s out, 0.24 s in, a 6 px rise). Chrome and Edge 126+, Safari 18.2+; elsewhere an ordinary navigation.
  • speculation rules: Tactical prerenders /light on hover or press (moderate), Light prerenders /m only on the press (conservative — Light is the 2G page); the page is usually ready when the thumb has finished.
  • a prerendered page is not a visit until it is shown: it opens no live line and sends no ping (whenShown() waits for prerenderingchange), and the server does not count a load that carries Sec-Purpose: prefetch.

Nothing is carried between the two: both read the same places, language and settings from the phone. With "reduce motion" the thumb does not slide and the pages do not fade.

1.42: flipping, not loading. When the other version is the page just before or just after this one in the history — the reader came from it — swNav() goes back (or forward) to it instead of loading it again (navigation.back() / forward(), only where the Navigation API can say which page that entry is; the paths /, /k, /kyiv count as /m, /l as /light). The browser keeps the page it left in memory (back/forward cache), so after the first switch both versions flip at once, as they were left. To be kept there a page must hold no open connection: both close their live line on pagehide (when the page is being kept) and open it again on pageshow (persisted) — Tactical with connect() and a pulse(true), Light with connectLive() and refresh(true) — so what changed meanwhile arrives at once. The pages are served no-cache with an ETag, never no-store, which would keep them out of that memory too.

10.3c The replay (1.42)#

A past window on the Tactical map, like a weather-radar loop: the ⟲ button on the map (and "Replay — the last 7 days" in Layers) opens a bar at the foot of the map with the window (the last 24 h, or one of the last seven nights, each 12:00 → 12:00 Kyiv time), a strip of the reports per quarter-hour with the time Kyiv city or oblast was under an official alert, a slider, −10′ / ▶ / +10′ and the speed (2, 10 or 30 minutes of replay per second). It starts ten minutes before the attack (the first ten minutes with at least a quarter of the busiest ten minutes' reports). /m#replay opens it at once (for a presentation). static/replay.js is downloaded only when it is opened. 1.54.7 (the owner: "an obvious loading spinner — people think the feature simply does not work"): while a window loads (up to half a minute the first time), a spinner turns over the map with the words (#rpwait, role=status, "the night is being read again from its posts") and in the bar, the slider shimmers and the controls wait greyed out (#rpbar.loading, waiting()).

  • The alerts: GET /api/replay rebuilds them as intervals from the start / end events (§13) — the alerts table cannot answer "what was on at 02:14": it keeps one row per region and alert type, and a region's second alert overwrites its first. A restart loses the alerts the process held without writing an end (the first reading after it writes a start for everything still on, and nothing for what ended while it was down), so a restart — recorded in boots since 1.41, and before that seen as a start for a region already on — closes every interval not started again within five minutes, at the restart. The page paints a moment's alerts with the live map's own function (alertMap() / applyAlertMap()), only when they change; the live colours are not painted while it is on.
  • The targets: the stored posts of the window are read with today's rules — _marks_from_posts() and _chain(), the live map's own functions — so a reading fixed since is fixed in the replay too; scope as on the map; no outcome. Each target stands where the latest post before that moment put it, until the next post moved it or its time ran out (the live clocks: a drone fades after 5 min and goes at 15, a missile at 12, an unspecified sign at 5), with its reported positions of the last 20 minutes as a trail — a leg longer than the target could have flown between the two posts is two targets joined, and the trail stops there. Nothing is extrapolated; a course is drawn only where a post stated one.
  • While it is on nothing live and nothing personal is on the map — no live mark, history, heat, bridge, watched place, pin or ring (for a screen shared in a presentation); the band and the card at the top stay live (the card folds to its line unless something near matters). The bar says "official alerts, and the targets where the channels' posts put them — not radar".
  • Server: a window of at most 26 h within the last 7 days, in whole minutes; built once an hour for a window in the past, once a minute for one that reaches now; twelve windows kept (replay_cached()). About 12 kB gzipped a day.

11. Privacy and security#

  • The four places never leave the phone; the Light page's requests carry no place (checked in the render tests).
  • 1.56: a street or a village is searched on the phone (static/streets.js); the server sees only which oblast's list was opened (/static/streets/<uid>.json, the Kyiv pair by default) — what the map tiles a phone shows already say, and less — never what is typed, nor the street chosen.
  • 1.57: the nearest shelter is found on the phone (static/shelters.js), from one file that is the same for everyone (/static/shelters.json): no pin and no position is in any request.
  • Push subscriptions: a ~10 km cell only; older stored homes are coarsened at start.
  • The Telegram session (TG_SESSION) is a key to a whole Telegram account. It is created on the owner's PC by scripts/telegram-login.bat and goes straight into Fly's secret store (fly secrets import, read from stdin): never on a command line, in a file, in the logs, in config.json or in the repository. Revoke it in Telegram: Settings → Devices → "Clear Sky server" → Terminate.
  • Usage counters (usage, usage_seen): a daily salted hash, no IP address, no location, no per-person history. A page the browser loads ahead of a tap (prerender, Sec-Purpose: prefetch) is not counted (1.40).
  • Flags and the corpus review are behind ADMIN_KEY.
  • The AI reader (1.50, §7.7) sends OpenRouter only public posts (the channel's text, its name, our place names), a card-like run of digits masked; never anything about a reader. Its key (OPENROUTER_API_KEY) is set by the owner with scripts/set-openrouter-key.bat, straight into Fly's secrets. Its readings are behind ADMIN_KEY.
  • The weather (1.33) asks Open-Meteo for seven fixed points (six launch areas and Kyiv) — nothing about a reader.
  • No secret is committed: config.json, vapid.json, .env, *.pem, databases and .flyapp are git-ignored; the history was scanned for tokens before the repository was published.
  • Static files are served gzipped with an ETag (Cache-Control: no-cache): a phone revalidates instead of downloading again, and still picks up a new version immediately.

15. Deployment and the GitHub repository#

  • The working folder is Desktop\clear-sky-main on the owner's PC: a Git clone of the private repository Dread92/clear-sky. Patches are committed there.
  • Shipping a patch: double-click scripts\release.bat. It refuses to run if anything is not committed, deploys to Fly.io, pushes to GitHub, and prints the live /api/version — the version shown must be the new one. The image is built by Fly's own builder (--depot=false); if that deploy fails — its builder machine sometimes does not answer ("failed to get status … unable to upgrade to h2c, received 500") — it tries once more with Fly's other builder, Depot (1.41.4). GitHub is pushed only after a deploy succeeded.
  • First deployment, or changing a key: deploy-fly.bat (Windows) — sign-in, app, volume, the dashboard key, the optional DeepL key, deploy. Details in DEPLOY.md. fly deploy -a kyiv-air-watch-gb alone also works.
  • GitHub: scripts\github-push.bat creates the private repository <you>/clear-sky with the GitHub CLI (sign-in in the browser; no token is ever typed or stored by the script) and pushes; run again to push new commits. Optional auto-deploy: add the repository secret FLY_API_TOKEN and fly-deploy.yml deploys every push to main.

When the app "goes off"#

Four causes look the same from a phone, and the dashboard (Live → Server, 1.41) tells them apart:

  • a release — a start with a new build in the list of starts: every deploy restarts the one machine (the database is on its volume), a few seconds to half a minute without answers. Deploy outside attacks.
  • a crash or an out-of-memory kill — "starts without a new version" above zero; scripts\diagnose.bat shows the machine's events (exit_code, oom_killed) and the last logs.
  • throttling — "taken by the host (steal)" above ~10 %, late wake-ups, slow requests: the shared vCPU has spent its burst balance and runs at its 6.25 % baseline (Fly's CPU quota). Remedy: a bigger machine, or less work per minute. Since 1.41.1 production runs on a dedicated CPU (performance-1x, 2 GB), which has no burst balance to spend: steal should stay near 0. 1.46 removed the work that most likely caused it (§5.5): once an attack night has been watched on the dedicated CPU with 1.46, a shared machine (shared-cpu-4x, 1 GB: a 25 % baseline) may be enough again — at about a quarter of the cost. The size is [[vm]] in fly.toml — fly deploy resets the machine to it, so a fly scale vm without editing the file lasts only until the next release.
  • a slow piece of work — slow requests on one path with normal steal; the watchdog's stack dump in the logs names it.

scripts\diagnose.bat (double-click on the owner's PC) writes fly status, the machines and their event logs, the recent logs and 20 timed pings to diag\fly-diag.txt (git-ignored): no key, no token, nobody's address.

17. Known limitations and open items#

  • chyste_nebo is read through the Telegram API with a user session. Telegram can restrict an account that reads channels by program; a dedicated account keeps the owner's own out of that risk. Its post formats have not been seen yet (the preview never showed them): check the first live posts and tune the parser.
  • siren.pp.ua is a volunteer proxy of the official API; an official key is more robust (§16).
  • Without a DeepL / Google key, English is the offline glossary and French shows English.
  • Ukrainian declension of place names after a preposition ("біля …") is not implemented; labels avoid it.
  • outcomes is written but nothing reads it yet (marker_log and channel_stats feed the dashboard's health view).
  • About 265 corpus cases are still pending review; the review left the dashboard in 1.19 and is done from the terminal (scripts/corpus.py, CORPUS.md) when needed.
  • Hromada alerts have no shapes of their own: on the Tactical map they colour their parent raion; on Light they are named ("alert in part of your raion").
  • 1.43: the regional villages come from GeoNames, whose Russian names are often transliterations and whose Ukrainian names are sometimes a renamed village's old one; a village GeoNames does not have (Cherkasy's Mytnytsia district) is not placed. chyste_nebocherkasy needs the Telegram API (its preview is off). Part II · The safety rules

Safety rules the code follows

People may look at this map while deciding whether to go to a shelter. That single fact decides most of the design. These rules are not style preferences — breaking one is a bug of the worst kind, and a pull request that breaks one will not be merged.

The app is never the primary warning#

The official Повітряна тривога app and the sirens come first, always. Heimdall adds detail to a warning that has already been given; it never replaces it. The disclaimer is shown on every open, not once. Since 1.36 the rotating ticker is gone and the essentials sit where they apply, without moving: "unofficial · not radar" at the foot of the card whenever it is open (a tap opens the full notice); "nothing reported within 30 km — that is not the same as safe, the siren decides" in the card whenever nothing is near; "do not publish air-defence or impact locations" at the top of the feed, where posts are read and shared.

No liberties with trajectories#

A target is drawn where a post said it was. Nothing else.

  • A Shahed wanders a figure of eight inside a dashed ring rather than sitting on a point, because its real path is erratic and a marker pinned to one spot would imply a precision nobody has. It flies that pattern nose-first, banking into its own turns. This is the one glyph on the map that turns, and it does not muddle what the map claims: the reported course is the chevron, pinned to the heading in the post. A moving aircraft reads as movement; a still one pointing somewhere nobody named would read as a bearing, which is the thing being avoided.
  • Dead reckoning along a heading exists only behind the explicit EST switch, off by default, labelled as an estimate. A drone that turned after the report would otherwise be drawn somewhere it is not. 1.45.1: the switch is suspended — hidden, and the estimate off for everybody — until it is reconsidered.
  • No ETA is ever displayed. "Arrives in 4 minutes" is a number the data cannot support, and someone would wait for it.
  • The course ray is capped and drawn with an uncertainty cone, never as a line to a destination.

Stale is never shown as active#

Silence is not safety, and it is not danger either — it is silence, and it must look like it.

Age without a new report What the map shows
< 5 min (track_stale_minutes) Live: coloured, animated, labelled
≥ 5 min Grey, a ? above the marker, "not updated for N min", animation stopped
≥ 8 min, ≥ 11 min Fades further, labels drop away
≥ 15 min (or the oblast's alert ends) Removed

A grey marker is a record of the last thing known, not a target's current position, and the sheet says so in those words.

A regional tracker's report (1.43.1) keeps on a longer clock — grey at 10 min, fainter at 15, "contact lost" at 20, removed at 25 (or at once when its oblast's alert ends). The Kyiv channels report a target every minute; the Chernihiv, Odesa or Kharkiv trackers every five to twenty, and their last report is the last thing anyone said. It is grey and labelled with its age for most of that time — never shown as a live position.

A missile is not aged like a drone#

The table above is written for a Shahed: about 3 km a minute, so a five-minute-old dot is still worth something, and going grey is an honest way to say this is the last thing anyone reported. Applying the same rule to a missile would be a lie with a precise pin in it. A Kalibr or a Kh-101 covers roughly 13 km a minute and turns as it goes; a ballistic covers around 35 and is on its terminal leg before a second report could exist. Two minutes of silence already puts the marker tens of kilometres from the truth.

So missiles are handled apart:

  • They never get the grey stale flag. There is no age at which "last seen here, not updated" is a useful thing to say about something moving that fast.
  • The pin becomes a circle. For a short window after the report — 2.5 min for cruise, 1.5 for ballistic — the position is still worth a point. Past that, the marker keeps the last reported position as its origin and grows a ring at the speed of that missile family, labelled could be anywhere within N km · last seen <place> at HH:MM. The ring is the claim; the point underneath it is only where the post put it. The ring is capped at 260 km, because past that it covers half the country and stops meaning anything.
  • They are dropped at 12 minutes (MISSILE_TTL_MIN), not 15. By then the last position tells you nothing at all, even as an uncertainty circle.
  • No course ray on a ballistic. A cruise missile flying a reported heading can justify a capped ray with a cone; a ballistic one on a lofted trajectory cannot, and the ray is hidden for that type.

Ballistic and cruise are also drawn differently on purpose — a white-hot fast-blinking spike against the red cruise arrow — because the time you have is different and the marker should say so at a glance, without being read.

Everything on the screen still comes from a post. Nothing here estimates where a missile is; the circle is an explicit statement of how little the last report now constrains it. It also keeps growing between server updates — at ballistic speed a circle that only moved when new data arrived would sit perfectly still exactly when it matters, and a still circle reads as a known position.

How often the app looks#

Refresh rate is a level, not a setting:

Level When Client Telegram and the alert APIs
0 nothing missile-shaped 15 s 30 s / 15 s
1 cruise missiles or a MiG-31K up 5 s 10 s
2 a ballistic threat is open 1 s 5 s

Both ends move together on purpose. A client polling every second in front of a server that last read its sources fifteen seconds ago is theatre: the app can only ever be as fresh as the slowest link, and that link is the source poll. Level 2 is rare and lasts minutes, which is the only reason a one-second cadence is affordable at all — and the ping counters are batched in memory rather than committed one by one, so counting usage can never be what stalls the alert path during an attack.

At that cadence the countdown is replaced by LIVE, because a number flickering between 1 and 0 reads as a fault rather than as speed. It turns red the moment a round actually fails, which is the only thing there worth noticing.

A new source is on trial until a person trusts it#

Channels can be added from the dashboard (1.31). An added channel starts on trial: its posts are shown in the feed, marked as such, and nothing else — no mark on the map, no banner, no sound, no push, no count. Only the admin, after reading what it posts, puts it on the map. A channel that starts posting rumours can be put back on trial or switched off in one click, and its posts leave the map at once.

A village is read only where it is (1.43)#

A regional tracker names villages, and a village's name is rarely unique: there are dozens of Іванівка in Ukraine and six in Odesa oblast alone. So the villages of an oblast are matched only in a post from that oblast's own tracker, or a sentence that names the oblast — a Kyiv channel's "Іванівка" is nothing. Inside the oblast, a name several villages share is placed only when something says which: another place the same post names, the channel's own report of the last half hour nearby, or a clear choice (the only city district, the only raion centre, by far the largest). With none of those, nothing is drawn — a mark in the wrong village is worse than no mark. A destination nobody can tell apart gets no course drawn to it. "In the area of A - B" is an area, never a route.

A shared text says what the post said, and nothing about who shares it (1.54.9)#

"Поділитися" put "19 km — Вишеньки" in the text: how far the target was from the reader's own pin, and the pin's place. Sent to a friend it is harmless; posted in a chat it tells strangers where somebody lives, works or has their children. A shared text now carries the kind, the place the post named, the time, a stated height and course, the channel and the reminder — never a pin, its name or a distance to it. The pins themselves are numbered and named by their owner, and the name stays on the phone with them.

A weapon nobody named is "probably a drone" — never "unknown", never "a drone" (1.54.8)#

A post that says where and not what ("Кулажинці", "лук'янівка") was shown as an unspecified threat. On a night of drones that word told people less than they knew: the owner, "it's most likely a drone (a jet Shahed); the only exception is when we know missiles are also coming". So the app and the AI's bulletins say probably a drone while no missile and no carrier is reported, and drone or missile while one is — the ⚠ stays, the card still says the type was not stated, and the bulletins never turn "probably" into a count of drones.

Two channels' reports are one target only when they could be (1.43.1)#

A report continues another channel's track only within 15 minutes and at a speed a drone can fly (1.54.7: the owner's physical check — 350 km/h for a Shahed, 650 for a jet drone, with 8 km for the places; a match that needs more is two targets, and a village sharing a name with a Kyiv district is never chosen if the drone could not have reached it); a report that names only the oblast never replaces one that named a place, and a destination is never handed to it. Joining two targets into one hides one of them — on 7 October the Kyiv channels' place names were taking over every Odesa and Chernihiv report within 475 km, and those oblasts' targets were not drawn at all. A drone or aircraft the post calls ours ("нашего БпЛА", "то наш") is not a target.

A post in Russian says the same thing (1.43)#

The Kharkiv and Odesa trackers write in Russian. Their posts are read through the same rules as Ukrainian ones, after their words are turned into the Ukrainian ones those rules know; nothing is added that the post did not say, and the text the reader sees is the post as written.

A regional warning is for that oblast (1.43)#

A regional tracker's ballistic or MiG-31K report raises the warning strip only for readers in that oblast — the one they picked, or one of their watched places. Kharkiv hears ballistic launches from Belgorod every day; Kyiv must not be told to take cover for each of them by a Kharkiv channel. The country-wide channels and the Air Force still warn everyone, and the official alerts are unaffected.

A missile post is for the oblasts it names (1.56.6)#

On 11 Oct 2026 the Air Force posted "🚀Швидкісна ціль на заході Донеччини, курс на північ." — a fast target in the west of Donetsk oblast, some 600 km from Kyiv. The app raised "ballistic — shelter now" for a reader in Vyshenky, and the drones around them turned into "drone or missile". A warning that cries wolf teaches people to ignore the next one. Now a missile post that names oblasts — written out, or through the towns it names — warns only the readers of those oblasts (Kyiv city counts as its oblast). A post that names none ("загроза балістики з північного сходу") still warns everyone: when the post does not say where, the app does not guess that it is elsewhere. A MiG-31K take-off warns everyone too. An unstated target is "drone or missile" only when a missile is reported within 150 km of it, or a missile warning stands for the reader's own oblast. The official alerts are never filtered: they come oblast by oblast already, and only the official data sets an alert.

A shelter is the city's, a distance is a straight line (1.57)#

The shelters on the map and on the card are the ones Kyiv city lists (KMDA), as the city describes them — not a list Heimdall made, checked or ranks by how safe it is. So: every shelter says what it is (metro, underpass, civil-defence shelter, dual-use structure, simplest shelter — a basement or a parking) and who may come in when, in the city's words: a school's shelter that is for its pupils and staff only, or one under repair, is never offered as "the nearest". A distance is a straight line, never a route or a walking time, and there is no countdown to reach it: going outside during an attack can be more dangerous than staying in, so every list ends with "Heimdall does not check shelters: one may be closed, locked or full" and "if you cannot get there in time: two walls between you and the street, away from windows". The nearest one is worked out on the phone; no pin leaves it.

The nuclear event cannot be switched on by accident#

The dashboard can cover the map on every page with the radiation symbol (1.32). A false one would be the worst thing this app could ever show — people leaving shelters, or panicking in a city at night — so it sits behind three locks: folded away in a danger zone; armed first, which makes the server issue a one-time code valid two minutes with one attempt, right or wrong; then the code and the phrase NUCLEAR EVENT (or ЯДЕРНА ПОДІЯ) typed in full and the button held for three seconds. It is never tested on the live service: the dashboard's Preview shows what the readers would see, on that screen only. It is for a nuclear or radiological emergency confirmed by the authorities or by certain evidence, never a rumour in a channel. Its banner says the app is unofficial and sends the reader to ДСНС and the local authorities; the prefilled advice (go indoors, close up, iodine only on official instruction) is the authorities' standard advice and is meant to be edited. It pulses once every 1.6 s — a flash rate that can trigger seizures is three a second — and holds still for anybody whose phone asks for less motion. It covers the map without blocking it: the live marks underneath still work.

A warning is only raised when something was actually reported#

The preventive banner (MiG-31K airborne, ballistic threat) is the loudest thing in the app, so what raises it is deliberately narrow:

  • Raises it: an official alert carrying that threat type; a post that says a launch was recorded (пуск, запуск Іскандер/Кинджал), a fast target (швидкісна ціль), ballistics named against a city, or a MiG-31K take-off.
  • Never raises it: a nightly assessment — "Загальна оцінка загроз на ніч…", "#обстановка", "Запуск може відбутись у будь-який момент", "ймовірні додаткові запуски". These are forecasts. They are tagged forecast, stripped of their threat tags on the server, and filtered again on the client. Tested in tests/test_tagging.py. What the enemy is said to plan ("ворог планує запустити …") is a forecast too (1.41.2).
  • Never raises it, never draws anything: a live tracker explaining rather than reporting — an example ("до прикладу — напрямок Бровари …"), a lesson ("розповім про балістику"), the channel describing its own method ("якщо ціль знищена над Києвом …"). On 6 Oct 2026 such an example became a ballistic missile at Brovary and the region's "shelter now" push. Tested in tests/test_talk_about_the_sky.py.
  • The banner cancels itself as soon as a later post lifts it (відбій, посадка, "загроза минула") and never outlives 20 minutes without a new report.

The same logic applies to markers: an announcement of an alert never becomes a target on the map, and a missile is only drawn if a place or a heading was actually reported.

Alert colours must match the official app#

If the government app shows an oblast under alert, this map shows the whole oblast under alert. An oblast-wide alert always paints the oblast; raion-level alerts can only add to it (a red raion inside a yellow oblast), never reduce or replace it. Getting this wrong — showing "clear" where the state says "alert" — is the single most dangerous bug this app can have.

Altitude is read, never guessed#

No public source publishes target altitude. So the app shows it only when a post states it in words, and shows nothing at all otherwise — never a default, never an estimate, never "level flight".

What the channels do say is read and shown:

The post says The map shows
знижується, зниження, заходить на ціль ↓ DESCENDING — the marker turns crimson and keeps its label at every zoom
набирає висоту climbing
низько, на малій висоті low
на висоті 2000м ~2000 m

A descending drone is the most dangerous state there is: it is diving at something. Until version 1.1 the word зниження was matched by the shoot-down keywords, so a drone in its attack dive was drawn green as "confirmed shot down". It is now a live target, and a post has to say збито / знищено before anything is called a shoot-down. tests/test_geo.py pins both halves of that.

A height is shown for 4 minutes at most (1.56.2). The owner: "a drone at 900 m whose altitude was last reported 10 minutes ago is probably already down, has struck a target, has climbed again or has changed path" — Kyiv INFO, 15:48 "900 m Shahed drone", 15:50 "explosions, close by", and the map still said 900 m at 15:59. A height, a descent and a run of heights are drawn on the maps and in the lists only while the report that stated them is at most four minutes old; after that the target has no height on the map. The card still tells it, as history: "900 m: «…» — reported 11 min ago, too old to be its height now", and its track keeps each report's height with its time.

A threat type is never invented#

If the post does not say what is flying, the map does not say it either: the marker is an amber warning triangle labelled type not stated, counted apart from drones and missiles. "Васильків увага ‼️" tells you where to worry, not what to worry about, and a Shahed icon there would be a claim nobody made.

Its sign stands upright and blinks red, and it never turns with the reported heading — a warning sign that is rotated 200° reads as a decoration, not a warning; the heading is shown by the chevron beside it. It lives five minutes and then disappears outright, with none of the grey fading a drone track gets: there is no target type to keep half-remembered.

The channels' shorthand does count as naming it — 🛸 🛵 🏍 🅿 for a strike drone, 🚀 for a missile, 💣 for a KAB — because that is how those channels write, every night.

A fire is not an explosion, and it does not last all night#

A burning roof reported an hour after a raid is a consequence somebody saw, not a strike. Drawing it as the red starburst inflates the explosion count and puts a strike marker where no strike was reported. So a post that says only fire — пожежа, горить, загорання — gets its own small amber flame, drawn quietly, counted in its own column and never added to explosions. The rule sits below the impact rule on purpose: "внаслідок влучання виникла пожежа" is a strike that started a fire and reads as a strike, and a shoot-down whose debris is burning stays a shoot-down. The more serious reading always wins.

A fire also leaves the live map after an hour (FIRE_TTL_MIN). After that it is either out or it has been burning all night, and neither belongs on a live map as though it had just happened. A new post about the same place brings it back, because that post carries a fresh timestamp.

A carrier is never a mark#

A Tu-95, a Tu-160, a Tu-22M3 or a MiG-31K is over Russia, Crimea or the sea when the channels report it. Any Ukrainian place name in such a post is something else — "борти Ту-160 з «Українки»" is an airbase in the Amur region, and it was drawn as strategic aviation 40 km south of Kyiv. Their posts raise their banners; they place nothing on the map (1.33).

A reply is its own message#

On the channels' web pages a reply shows the message it answers first. Read carelessly, the Air Force's "all clear for the MiG-31K" became a second take-off at the very minute the danger was called off. A post is read as what it says itself, never as what it quotes.

What was carried is as old as where it came from#

A live tracker's next line often says only where the target is going ("На Таращу") or what happened to it ("Вибух"). The app places such a line at the channel's last reported position — and until 1.38 it gave that old position the new line's time. On 5 October a jet drone destroyed at Vydubychi at 20:25 stood there as a live target, "1 min ago", until 20:37, carried by lines about another target 100 km south; it lit two bridges. Now a carried position keeps the time it was reported at and goes grey, then away, on that clock; a line saying the target fell, was lost or that the sky is clear ends it; and the official all-clear for Kyiv city ends everything reported in the city before it. Telegram still cannot end an alert; the official all-clear can end what Telegram said before it.

A bridge with nothing reported is not a safe bridge#

The bridge alerts (1.34) say what the channels reported on or near each bridge, from the places they named — a district is a kilometre wide, and a post comes a minute or more after the sighting, in which a jet drone covers 6 km. So a bridge is "target at the bridge", "target 2.3 km away", "hit reported" — or nothing. Nothing is never written as "clear" or "safe", the texts say "from channel posts, not radar", and the official alert still comes first. A post that names only the city, or a bank of the river, is 2–4 km from several bridges at once: it lights none.

"Hit reported" is said only when a post names the bridge and says the bridge was hit (влучання, пошкоджено, приліт). An explosion near a bridge is not a hit: most explosions over Kyiv are interceptions. On 5 October a bare "Вибух", placed at the tracker's last position near two bridges, made "hit reported" on both for half an hour; nobody had reported a hit, and the app does not draw that conclusion for anyone (1.38).

The card says what is known about your place, and nothing more#

The card at the top of the Tactical page (1.36) is the first thing read, so it is held to the strictest rules. Its colour block is the official status of the watched place's own raion — the government app's data, by raion, the same rule as the Light page — and each place's chip carries only that. The target under it is one the map already shows, chosen by a fact: a post said it is heading for the place, or it is the nearest reported within 30 km; its distance is between two reported points, never a projection, and no time of arrival is ever written. When nothing is near it says so, and that this is not the same as safe. The escalation level that colours its edge and picks the tone (0–4) is computed from those same facts — an official alert, a reported distance, a bridge "at", a ballistic or MiG-31K warning — never from a guess about where something is going. The one instruction it gives, under a fresh ballistic or MiG-31K warning ("little time — go to a shelter now, do not wait for the siren"), is the guide's own rule, taken from the official guidance, with the guide one tap away. The card sits in the header, in the page's flow: it never covers the map.

The reader may fold it to one line (1.37), because on a quiet night a third of a phone screen spent on "nothing reported" is a third of the map nobody can read. The folded line still says the place's official status, and the card opens by itself — not on the reader's next tap — when something new matters: the level rises above the one it was folded at, or a preventive warning or a bridge bar appears; while a fresh warning is on, the line is that warning. Folding hides detail, never the official status and never a new warning.

The heat map is statistics, and says so#

Where drones were reported (1.34) is a record of the past, under every mark, with "not live, not alerts" in its legend. Since 1.40 its colour says how many reports a place had — pale yellow for a few, red for the most — because a heat map people can read at a glance is worth more than one they have to decode. Red and yellow are also the alert colours, so two rules keep them apart: the heat map is soft discs around places, never a filled raion; and while it is on, the official alerts are a faint tint under it (1.54.2: no longer outlined — a night of yellow-level alerts drew a yellow mesh over every raion), and its legend says so. A place named once or twice paints nothing (1.54.2): the map shows where reports gather, not every village a post ever named. The card above the map still gives the place's official status in words. It counts the places posts named for drones and unidentified targets — never the shoot-downs or the hits, which would map where the air defence works. It is off at every opening of the app: nobody should open the map at 3 a.m. and see last week.

A ballistic report is not an official alert, and a retraction ends it#

On 7 Oct 2026 at 01:14 three channels relayed a ballistic launch "towards Kyiv" that the military's own system had shown by mistake (a speed of 6000, "probably a system lag"). Kyiv city was never under an official alert; the app said "shelter now, do not wait for the siren" for eight minutes and drew the missile "0 km from Kyiv". Now: with no official alert over the reader's place, the warning says exactly that and asks the reader to be ready and to go at the siren, then turns grey after four minutes; a mark at the town a missile is heading for is "heading for", never a position; and a channel's retraction ("лаг системи", "не підтвердився пуск") ends the channels' ballistic marks and warnings of the 20 minutes before it — never an official alert, which only the official data ends.

The replay is the past, and looks like it#

The replay (1.42) puts a past night on the map. It must never be read as the present, nor say more about the past than the posts did. So: a red "REPLAY" tag, the replayed date and time in large type, and the bar under the map for as long as it is on; nothing live on the map meanwhile (no live mark, history, heat or bridge) — and the band at the top stays live, so an alert that starts during a presentation is still seen. The alert colours are the official ones of that moment. A target stands where a post put it — never moved along a course, never joined into a route across a leap no drone could fly — and points only where a post stated a course; outcomes are not drawn. The posts are read with today's rules, so a reading corrected since (a lesson read as a missile, "next: Nyvky" read as a shoot-down) is corrected in the replay too. Nothing personal is drawn — no watched place, pin or ring — because the replay is made to be shown.

The guide says what the official guidance says#

The what-to-do page (1.35, /guide) is the one place where the app tells people how to act, so it carries nothing of its own invention. Every rule is taken from guidance the ДСНС, the National Police, the Ministry of Health or an oblast administration published, and those sources are listed on the page; what is not taken from them is said there too (the bridge advice applies the police's advice for drivers to the bridges; a card with a parent's number in a child's pocket is common sense). Warning times and speeds are written as typical values, never as a promise — "2–4 minutes" for a ballistic missile is what it usually is, not what it will be. It never says that a place, a floor or a bridge is safe — only what is safer, and what to avoid. And it puts the official siren first, in its opening lines.

When the official guidance changes, the page changes with it, and its "checked" date moves.

The history layer never buries the live picture#

The map carries the last 24 hours and nothing more. Beyond that the dots are denser than the thing somebody opened the app to see, and a person deciding whether to go to a shelter should not have to read three days of history to find what is flying now. Longer windows — 24 h, 72 h, 7 days — live in a report behind the counter chips, grouped by place: "Kyiv ×12, last at 03:41" says what two hundred dots were trying to say. The report repeats, under its own figures, that they count posts and not events.

1.54, the owner: the layer (the ✸ button, 24 hours of explosions and shoot-downs) "does not help much for now" and is gone, with its report. The live outcomes stay on the map, on their own short clocks (see "An explosion is a moment, not a state"). Should a history layer come back, this rule and the next ones are what it has to meet.

What was destroyed decides what it means#

"Склад гуманітарного фонду … знищено" is a warehouse on the ground. Read as a shoot-down it became a green tick over a strike site — the opposite of what happened — and it padded the interception count with somebody's ruined building. A destruction word only reads as a shoot-down when the sentence is about something that was flying (ціль, БпЛА, шахед, ракета). Otherwise it is damage on the ground: its own muted marker, its own column, never added to shoot-downs. "Збито 5 БпЛА, уламки пошкодили будинок" is still a shoot-down; debris damaging a roof, with nothing said about what was intercepted, is damage.

A press release is not an observation of the sky#

"Уряд розширив програму страхування воєнних ризиків … за пошкодження або знищення якого можна отримати компенсацію" is a government announcement about insurance. It put a damage marker over Kyiv and — worse — closed a live Shahed track with it, because anything carrying an outcome was allowed to end a flight.

Three rules now:

  • An article produces no outcome of any kind. Policy vocabulary — уряд, кабмін, законопроєкт, страхування, компенсація, відшкодування, бюджет, пільговий кредит, млн/млрд грн — never appears in somebody reporting what is overhead, and neither does a post of more than 700 characters. Such a post produces no marker and no status, whatever destruction words it contains. The guard covers every status: a visit to a new fire station drew a fire marker beside Kyiv because the fire rule ran before the news check.
  • A fire needs a fire, not a fire station. The root пожеж is in the name of every fire service, engine and brigade in the country, so it cannot by itself mean something is burning. A fire is only drawn when the sentence says one happened — виникла / сталася / спалахнула пожежа, пожежа в …, горить, займання. A post that says "Пожежі попередньо немає" draws no fire — and still keeps the damage marker its text earns, because rejecting the fire reading falls through to the next rule rather than abandoning the post.
  • Only an outcome that ends a flight may close a track: shot down, arrived, lost from tracking, area declared clear. Damage and fire never close anything — they are what a strike left on the ground, and a burning roof says nothing about whether the drone above it is still flying. A live target must never vanish from the map because of something that happened underneath it.

The second rule matters more than the first. A missed damage marker costs a user some information; a live target removed from the map by a press release is the failure this whole document exists to prevent.

A name that merely starts the same is not a match#

Stemming strips trailing vowels, so Коломия was indexed as колом — which quietly swallowed Коломак, a different town 700 km east. Five Shaheds were drawn over Ivano-Frankivshchyna from a post about Kharkiv oblast. Two guards now stand there:

  • When the stem gave up two or more letters, the matched word must still agree with the full name one character past the stem. коломиї does; коломак does not. Alternation spellings (фастів→фастов, Київ→києв) are explicit entries and are trusted as written, so no real declension was lost.
  • An oblast adjective is not the Kyiv place of the same name. "Житомирська" is a metro station on Kyiv's red line and how every post names Zhytomyr oblast. Read as the station, a forestry post about Olevsk — 150 km away — planted a marker 9 km from Kyiv. When an oblast noun follows the word (область, обласна рада, ОВА), the word is the oblast and nothing matches.
  • A marker's oblast is the oblast of the place it actually matched, never the one the sentence mentions. The Kolomyia marker carried Kharkiv's oblast id and Ivano-Frankivsk's coordinates at once; that contradiction was visible inside the app before it was visible on the map.

A stated part of an oblast is not the oblast centre#

"Реактивні БпЛА на півночі … Київщини" was dropped on the oblast centre, which for Kyiv oblast sits near Vasylkiv — in the south, on the wrong side of the city from the reported drones. When a post names a part of an oblast, the marker goes there and the card says north of the oblast instead of oblast centre. The confidence stays low, because a quadrant is still not a position.

Only locative phrasing counts. "У західному напрямку" is a course, not a place, and never moves a marker — discarding a stated quadrant and inventing one are the same failure in opposite directions.

Some channels write that part where the town would go: "Одещина: ➡️Південь/Одеса". With no town to match, the line landed on the oblast centre — 90 km north of the south the channel had named, and on the same pixel as every other line of that shape during a raid. Those lines now place in the named part. Only exact compass words count: Південне is a town, not "the south", and a name that merely begins like a direction never moves a marker.

1.45: a part of an oblast is still not a point. Such a report is now drawn on the region like any report that names the oblast and no town — its outline, the weapon and the count — and its card says north of the oblast. And when the same sentence names the town it is heading for ("на півночі Чернігівщини ➡️ курсом на Славутич"), it is drawn as an approach to that town: the one place the post did name. A raion named as where it is ("залітає у Гайсинський район … курсом на Теплик") is the same: an approach to Теплик, with no course drawn from the raion's centre.

A side of a town is not the town (1.45.1)#

"БпЛА на півночі від Миколаєва" — north of Mykolaiv — was drawn on Mykolaiv itself, "±5 km, high confidence". The post named the city as a reference and gave no distance. Now: with a distance ("за 20 км на північ від …") the mark goes that far that way; from an oblast's capital without one, the oblast is marked, as for any report with no town, and the card says north of Mykolaiv; from any other town, the mark stays at the town with low confidence and the card says north-west of Brovary.

Where it is, and where it is going, are different facts#

"3х мгКР Бандероль у напрямку Ніжин. Далі Київщина" says the missiles are heading for Nizhyn and will carry on into Kyiv oblast. Reading "Київщина" as their position put a second marker at the centre of Kyiv oblast, 150 km from the only place the post actually named — one flight drawn twice, the second time over a region nothing had been reported in. An oblast introduced by далі / потім / згодом / надалі / курсом на is the route ahead and never a position.

The reverse also holds: when the post does name the oblast it is heading for, the card says which one. Writing "neighbouring oblast" threw away the one thing the channel had been precise about.

A glyph points where the post said, or it does not point at all#

The missile glyph was drawn with flared tail fins, and at marker size the eye takes the widest part for the head: a missile flying south-west read as an arrow pointing north-east. The ballistic glyph was worse — its spike was at the bottom, so once rotated by the reported course it pointed a clean 180° wrong. Both now carry their mass at the nose, so rotate(heading) means what it says.

A target reported with no course at all gets a mark with no front. A pointed glyph left at 0° reads as "heading north", which is an invented heading — the one thing this app must never publish.

A heading over a list is not a sighting#

The monitoring channels write a region on a line of its own and the sightings under it:

🛵 Київщина
- реактивний на Кагарлик
- 7 бандеролей на зону ЧАЕС повз Остер/Десна

The heading was being drawn as a report of its own, on the oblast centre — for Kyiv oblast that is near Vasylkiv, 100 km south of the Chornobyl zone the very next line was actually about. Every post of this shape produced one phantom marker in the middle of the region. A line that is nothing but an oblast name, with lines under it, now sets the context and draws nothing.

It has to be a whole line with something under it: "КАБи на Сумщину та Донеччину" is a list of two places, and its second half is a report in its own right.

The weapon belongs to the line that names it#

The threat type used to be resolved once for the whole post and applied to every line in it, so one word anywhere coloured everything: in the post above, the jet drone over Kaharlyk was drawn as a Banderol — a different weapon, at a different speed, with a different uncertainty ring. Each line is typed from its own words, and only a line that names no weapon inherits the post's.

A tally of the night is not a sky#

The Air Force's summaries are the one tally the app keeps (1.32): read weapon by weapon into its statistics and the dashboard's Analytics, never onto the map. A weapon the summary names without a number is shown as "used" — never counted as zero, and never given a number the Air Force did not give.

"В ніч на 17.09.26 … противник застосував … 8× балістичних ракет по Києву" counts what was fired last night. It was being drawn as eight ballistic missiles over Kyiv, right now, for the whole time the post stayed in the feed. A retrospective summary produces no markers at all — same rule as a press release, for the same reason.

One weapon, one symbol — and it never turns#

A symbol that rotates has to point somewhere, so a target reported without a course forced a choice between inventing a heading and drawing a second, different icon for the same weapon. The app did both at different times: a Banderol was an arrow in one place and a diamond in another, on the same map.

Air-defence displays settled this long ago. The symbol says what it is; the leader line says where it is going. Every weapon has one silhouette, drawn at the same fixed angle everywhere on the map — so its nose cannot be read as a course, because every marker's nose points the same way. The chevron, the ray and the uncertainty cone carry the whole direction story, and they are simply absent when the post gave no course. Nothing has to be invented, and nothing has to be explained away.

An outcome with no place named belongs to the region, not to its centre#

"Київщина - вибухи" names an oblast and no town. Drawn as a pin it landed on the oblast centre, which for Kyiv oblast is near Vasylkiv — so a report about a region of three million people read as an explosion in one village, at high precision, in the wrong place.

These no longer produce a marker at all. The region itself is outlined and tinted, and a label inside it says what was reported and that it was somewhere in the oblast. A label is not a position, and it cannot be mistaken for one.

1.45: the same for a target still in the air. "Чернігівщина: 3 реакти" drew three jet drones in the middle of Chernihiv oblast, a hundred kilometres from anything the post said, and somebody living near that point took it for a position. A target reported for a whole oblast is drawn on the region — its outline, the weapon's silhouette and the count — never as a pin; the Near list gives it no distance and it never raises the level; a push never says "within 15 km of you" about it. And a town it is heading for is never the oblast centre: "3 реакти на Славутич з півночі" under a Chernihiv heading is an approach to Slavutych, from the north — what the post said, and nothing more.

An explosion is a moment, not a state#

Outcome markers stayed on the live map for 25 minutes. During a raid that filled the map with bursts that had already finished, and a burst that is still drawn reads as still happening there. Ten minutes, then it belongs to the history layer — which is where you go to ask what happened, not what is happening. A fire keeps its hour, because a fire really does last.

In Chernihiv oblast a drone faces into Ukraine (1.56.3)#

A course the posts did not give is not drawn: no ray, no heading, no ETA. But a silhouette has to point somewhere, and upright means north — near Liubech, back to Belarus. The owner: "by default the drones must always face into Ukraine when no direction is given — it is extremely unlikely that a drone goes back to Belarus or Russia". So in Chernihiv oblast a drone with no course faces towards Kyiv, and its card says so ("drawn facing into Ukraine: no course was stated"). A course worked out from two of a channel's reports is kept only when each report was of one target (two lines of two posts are two targets, not a course), and never when it points back across the border. A course a post states is always drawn as stated.

"Heading to your village" is a thing the post has to say#

The priority location alert used to mean "the reported course passes within 25° of it". From 80 km away a 25° cone is 35 km wide, so the app told people a drone was coming to their village when the post had said nothing of the kind — the single most alarming sentence in the app, produced by arithmetic on a bearing.

It is now said only when the source said it: the target's place, or the destination the post named, is that town. Everything else gets a distance in kilometres, which is a fact, and nothing more.

A push that cannot be acted on costs the ones that can#

Siren-start and all-clear pushes are gone. "Kyiv oblast — alert" says nothing a person can do anything with: not what, not where, not how far — and the siren itself already said that much, louder. It still wakes somebody at three in the morning, and after enough of those the notification that does matter gets swiped away with the rest. What is left is what is specific: a MiG-31K or a ballistic launch, and a target actually near the reader.

The one row that could say where somebody sleeps#

A push subscription paired the address a phone can be reached at with the exact coordinates of the village its owner had chosen. Nothing in the app needs that: a proximity alert asks whether anything is within N km, and N is never smaller than 10. The point is snapped to a ~10 km cell and the name is dropped, existing rows are rewritten at startup, and the radius test is widened by half a cell so nobody is missed because their village was rounded.

The push text changed with it. "23 km from you" was precision the stored point no longer has, so the reader gets the radius they chose and the place the post named — both of which are true.

What the app said is kept, not just what it was told#

Markers were recomputed from the feed on every request, and the feed prunes. So "what did the map show at 02:14 last Tuesday" had no answer; neither did "how much warning did this give over the siren", which is the app's whole reason to exist. Every marker is now written once, as computed, with the evidence that produced it, and outcomes have a table of their own. The numbers on the statistics tab are measured against that record — not against anybody's claim, including this app's.

"High confidence" says how sharply it was read, not that it is true#

Confidence used to be one word with no stated meaning. Each reading method has a known sharpness, and the app now spells it out beside the word: a named town pins a position to ±5 km, an oblast name pins it to nothing smaller than the whole oblast, a bearing between two named places is good to about ±10°, a compass word to about ±30°.

These are the app's own reading tolerances. They are not a probability that the report is true: no channel publishes such a number, and this app does not invent one.

Nothing is drawn where the source said it is clear#

Posts mix an all-clear and a warning in one breath: "Чисте небо Київська область та Київ. Васильків увага ‼️". Clear sentences are removed before parsing, so only Vasylkiv gets a marker. A false alarm over a city that was just declared clear is the fastest way to make people stop trusting the map — and then stop reading it at all.

Overlapping explosions are grouped, never stacked#

At a 300 km view two explosions 3 km apart are the same eleven pixels. Drawing both put two glyphs on top of each other and two labels in the same space; pushing them onto a ring, the earlier fix, moved each dot off the place it was actually reported. Both are worse than one dot saying ×8.

So at draw time anything closer than one glyph-width merges into a single marker carrying the total:

  • The group is anchored and labelled by its most recent member, never by a centroid. A centroid puts a dot on a spot where nobody reported anything, which is exactly the kind of invented precision this map avoids.
  • Explosions and confirmed shoot-downs are never merged into each other. They mean opposite things, and are grouped separately even when they sit on the same street.
  • It is purely a function of zoom. Zooming in pulls the group apart into its separate reports, and a tap lists every one of them with its own place, time, channel and post — no single report ever stands in for the others.
  • The counts in the live tally and the statistics are computed before grouping, so what is on the screen never changes a number.

Every number is traceable#

Tap any marker and you see the sentence it came from, the channel, the time, and how position, heading and count were read, each with a confidence. If a value has no evidence, it is not shown. "Machine-read — always check the sentence" is on the sheet, because the source can be wrong too.

In the statistics, launches (from Air Force summaries) and reports (a count of posts) are never mixed and never added together. A number that counts posts is labelled as counting posts.

That labelling is not cosmetic. "Confirmed shot down: 310" reads as 310 things were shot down, which the app has no way to know: it counts the monitoring posts it could parse and tie to a named town. Every shoot-down that was never posted, posted without a place, or written in a form the parser missed is absent from it. So these figures are named explosion reports and shoot-down reports, and the caveat sits directly under the numbers rather than in a footnote at the bottom of the panel: what the app read, not official totals, and the real numbers are higher — never lower. A floor presented as a total is a false precision in the same family as an invented trajectory.

The AI writes bulletins, and puts nothing on the map (1.54)#

The owner (9 Oct 2026): "instead of all that on the map, summaries of the activity of the last hour / the last 24 hours, a bit like a weather bulletin — the AI placing things on the map, we forget it". So since 1.54 nothing the AI says reaches the map, the tags, the alerts, the feed or a push. It writes bulletins (TECHNICAL.md §7.11), and they are held to this document like everything else on the screen:

  • Written from the server's own record, never from a post: the official alerts, the targets the rules drew, the outcomes, the heights, the Air Force's figures — counts, place names and times. What it is given is stored with what it wrote, so every sentence can be checked against its record.
  • "Reported", always. Channel reports, not radar and not a count of drones; the card says "written by AI from the channels' posts and the official alerts — not radar".
  • Never "safe", never that the danger is over, never shelter advice, never a forecast — a trend the record shows ("fewer reports in the last 20 minutes") is a fact and may be said; "they will" is not.
  • Never where targets came from or where they are heading: the posts' origins were wrong too often to show on the map (1.53.2), and a bulletin is no place to bring them back. A followed target's reported places, in order, may be told.
  • Alerts are named only from the official data, and only the official data sets a colour — a bulletin starts and ends nothing.
  • Behind the dashboard's switch, off until the owner turns it on; and a bulletin the server can no longer stand by — the AI off, out of budget, failing — goes stale and leaves the app rather than describing an hour that has moved on.
  • A quiet window is said by the server itself, in fixed words, without asking the model anything.

The sections below describe the AI reading posts for the map (1.50–1.53); none of it runs any more.

A language model is a second opinion, never a source (1.50)#

The AI reader (TECHNICAL.md §7.7) reads the same posts as the rules: what it says is stored for the dashboard and compared with the rules, and it never places, moves or removes a mark, and never reaches the alerts, a banner or a push. 1.51, the owner's decision (9 Oct 2026), under a kill switch on the dashboard: the facts it quoted that the rules did not read — where a target comes from, its height, its speed — fill the target tags, marked as the AI's. A model writes fluent, confident answers whether or not the post supports them — exactly the confidently wrong marker this document exists to prevent. So it is held to the same rules as the parser, and checked rather than trusted: it may only state what the post states, with the post's own words as the quote for every fact; it may only name places this server offers it from its own gazetteer, by id, never coordinates; Banderol is a jet drone to it as to everything else; whatever the server cannot find in the post is dropped before anyone sees it. Its use is to show where the rules are wrong, so that the rules are fixed. Letting it place anything, ever, is a separate decision taken on its measured record — never a default, and never for a post the rules already read.

Only public posts are sent to it: a channel's text, its name, our place names. Never a reader, a watched place, an address or a device.

A target only the AI reader found (1.52)#

(Until 1.54 — no such target is drawn any more.)

The owner's decision (9 Oct 2026): a target missed is worse than a duplicate — a "ghost" ten kilometres from a real target is better than a target nobody drew because a post was worded loosely. So a target the AI reader found that the rules did not read is drawn, marked "AI" on the map and in its card (1.53: never pushed "near you" — see below). What does not change: it is placed only at a place the post itself names, one the server offered from its gazetteer — loose wording is read, a place is never made up; never at an official alert message's district; never at coordinates of the model's own. When the rules and the AI read a post's one target more than 30 km apart, both are drawn ("біля Сомкової Долини" was read by the rules as Dolyna, 500 km off). The dashboard's switch takes every such target off the map within ten seconds.

The AI reads every 10 minutes and writes up the night (1.53)#

(1.54: the 10-minute check is stopped; the report on each night stays, for the dashboard.)

The owner's decision (9 Oct 2026), on cost: the AI reads the posts of each ten minutes in one call, beside what the rules drew and what the map shows, and reports only where the rules are wrong or incomplete. The rules stay the reader of the map: what the AI finds fills the tags (quoted, checked against the post) and a target only it found is drawn, marked AI — but it can be ten minutes old, so it is never pushed "near you". A namesake is never a second target: where the post names one place, the rules' choice of it stands. Its notes and its report on each night are the dashboard's alone — counts and the places the posts named, "reported", never a forecast stated as a fact; they are there to find patterns and to fix the rules, not to tell anyone what to do.

A tag no longer changes under a target being followed: a track keeps the number it was last shown with, and a second channel's report of the same target at the same time takes the same number.

A target's tag is a number, not a designation (1.51)#

1.56.5: no number is shown in the app for now. The owner: "it doesn't work and it's very unreliable" (in 1.53 already: "the ID number changes during the course"). A number that is wrong is worse than none — it makes two drones look like one, or one look like two. The pages show none (TAGS_SHOWN); what follows describes them as the server still keeps them, for the dashboard and the record.

1.53.2: the tag is the number alone — "#07". Where a target came from is no longer shown anywhere a reader sees: read from the posts, it was wrong too often (a track takes the first origin any of its reports stated, often another target's), and a wrong "from the east" over a drone coming from Belarus is worse than nothing. What follows describes the letter as it is still kept, unseen, for the record.

"S-07 ×2" is Heimdall's own count of the targets the channels reported tonight, and a letter for where a post said it came from — N north or Belarus, E east or Russia, S south, the sea or Crimea, W west, ? not said. The letter is not worked out from a position, with one exception the owner made (1.52): a target first reported in Chernihiv oblast is "probably from Belarus", one first reported in Cherkasy oblast "probably from the south or the east" — shown in lower case (n-07) and said to be a guess in the card; a stated origin always replaces it. "з Чернігівщини" is a place it passed, not an origin. A target reported for a whole oblast gets no tag at all: a number only when it is sure which target it is. The bottom line (height, speed) is only what the target's latest report stated. The format is Heimdall's, not a military one — the card says so — because a tag that looks like an air-defence designation would be read as coming from air defence. The rules' reading comes first; until 1.54 the AI's filled what the rules did not read, marked "AI" — since 1.54 a tag is the rules' reading alone.

A target drawn at the town it is heading for ("2 реактиви у бік Києва з півдня") is not at that town. Its glyph stands off the town on the side the post named, a fixed distance on the screen — not a distance on the ground, which nobody stated — and its card says where it is now was not given.

A pin on a street, found on the phone (1.56)#

The pin pickers search every street and village of Ukraine (OpenStreetMap, © its contributors, ODbL) so that a pin can be set where its owner really is — a street in Brovary, not the town's centre 3 km away, which can put a 10 km zone on the wrong side of a village. The search runs on the phone: the server sends the oblast's whole list and never learns what is typed or which street was chosen — only which oblast's list was opened, which the map tiles a phone shows already say. The list is OpenStreetMap's, not an official register: a street missing or misplaced there is missing or misplaced here, so the pin is shown on the map at the street and its owner can move it to the exact spot.

Where drones usually go is the dashboard's, not a reader's (1.55)#

The owner wants the foundations of a probability: "the % that a drone goes to X, Y or Z", area by area, accumulated night after night. The dashboard's Patterns tab has it — where the next report put the targets seen in an area, where the tracks that passed through it were last reported, each share with its 95% range — and the app has none of it, by the owner's decision: no page asks for it, the route is 🔒.

A pattern is what targets did on other nights, not what this one will do. Shown beside a live target it would read as its course — the trajectory a post did not state (the first rule of this file). If it ever reaches the app, it is the owner's call, said as a history ("on past nights, most targets reported here were next reported over …"), with its number of nights and its range, never as an arrow on a target, a destination, an ETA or a reason to stop sheltering. And it is the channels' reports, not radar: a move is one report to the next, so a target nobody reported between two towns is one straight move between them.

Operational security#

The app does not encourage publishing air-defence positions or explosion locations during an attack — the disclaimer and the top of the feed say so explicitly. The history layer only shows what was already public, only at the level of a named town, and only after the fact.

When in doubt#

Show less. A missing marker costs a user some information. A confidently wrong marker, a false all-clear, or an invented trajectory can cost something else.

Appendix A · Deployment

Deploying

Fly.io (what this repo is set up for)#

One performance-1x machine (a dedicated CPU, 2 GB) in Amsterdam with a 1 GB volume for the history — about $34 a month in Amsterdam (Fly's price list, Oct 2026). Until 1.41.1 it was a shared-cpu-1x / 256 MB machine (a few dollars), whose CPU Fly throttles once its burst is spent: the pages timed out on busy nights. The size is [[vm]] in fly.toml; fly deploy applies it. Fly requires a payment method on the account.

First time#

Windows: double-click deploy-fly.bat — it installs flyctl, logs you in, creates the app and the volume, asks for the dashboard key (ADMIN_KEY) and, optionally, a DeepL key for EN/FR translation, then deploys.

Manually, anywhere:

fly auth login
fly apps create clear-sky --org personal
fly volumes create data --size 1 --region ams --yes -a clear-sky
fly secrets set ADMIN_KEY=a-long-random-string -a clear-sky     # protects /admin only; the map stays public
fly secrets set DEEPL_KEY=your-deepl-key -a clear-sky            # optional: clear EN/FR translation
fly secrets set UKRAINEALARM_KEY=your-key -a clear-sky           # optional: official API instead of the proxy
fly deploy -a clear-sky --ha=false

Change app = "…" in fly.toml to your app name.

The satellite layer (once)#

The satellite imagery is a file on the volume, not part of the image. Build it with python scripts/build_imagery.py imagery/sat-ua.mbtiles (needs pip install rasterio numpy pillow scipy matplotlib; zoom 13, the full 10 m: ~1.5 h, reads every cell at full resolution from the public ESA bucket, makes 406 MB — --maxzoom 12 is ~45 minutes and ~130 MB), then double-click scripts/upload-imagery.bat: it shows the volume's free space and uploads the file (fly ssh sftp put). Without the file the map simply does not offer the layer. Every new build takes a new --version (the default is 2): the browsers keep the tiles a year under that version.

The upload goes next to the old file before replacing it, so the volume needs ~0.45 GB free for the zoom-13 file (upload-imagery.bat shows it first; the 1 GB volume has it). If not: fly volumes list -a kyiv-air-watch-gb, then fly volumes extend <volume id> -s 2 -a kyiv-air-watch-gb (once; $0.15 per GB a month).

The closest zooms (1.48, optional): Esri World Imagery from zoom 14. Create an account on ArcGIS Location Platform (location.arcgis.com), an API key with the Basemaps privileges (both: basemap styles and static basemap tiles — 1.49's streets use the second) and no referrer restriction (the server asks Esri, not the browser), then double-click scripts/set-esri-key.bat and paste it. 2 M tiles a month are free, then $0.15 per 1,000; the server stops at esri_monthly_tiles (1.8 M) and a tenth of it a day. The month's count is on the dashboard. Without the key the satellite layer is Sentinel alone, enlarged when zoomed in.

The AI reader (1.50, optional): a language model reads the channels' posts beside the rules, in shadow mode — nothing it says reaches the map; the dashboard's AI reader tab shows where it and the rules disagree. Create an account on openrouter.ai, add a few dollars of credit, create an API key with a credit limit (10 dollars), then double-click scripts/set-openrouter-key.bat and paste it. The server also stops itself at ai_reader_monthly_usd (10 dollars a month, config.json). Only public post text is sent.

TG_API_ID, TG_API_HASH, TG_SESSION optional Read chyste_nebo through the Telegram API. Set by scripts\telegram-login.bat, never by hand.

Its own address (domain)#

Fly cannot rename an app, and a new *.fly.dev name would be one more move for everybody who installed the app. Give it a real domain instead (for example heimdall.com.ua, open to anyone, ~700 UAH a year):

  1. Buy the domain at any registrar.
  2. Double-click scripts\set-domain.bat. It adds the free HTTPS certificates on Fly (fly certs add), shows the DNS records to create at the registrar (A and AAAA for the domain, CNAME for www), and checks the certificate.
  3. When the certificate is issued, the script offers to set CANONICAL_HOST: pages still opened on kyiv-air-watch-gb.fly.dev then show "Heimdall has moved to …" with a button to the new address. The old address keeps working; the reader's settings follow in the link (never the watched places — they are set again there). An installed app has to be added to the home screen again from the new address: a browser ties an installed app to its address, nothing can move it.

Appendix B · Languages

Translations (EN / UK / FR)

Every string the user can see lives in static/i18n.js. The page is fully translated in English, Ukrainian and French — including place names, raions, Kyiv districts, rivers, airports and channel names.

How it works#

t('d_ok')                       // → "I understand" / "Зрозуміло" / "J'ai compris"
t('lbl_to', 'Kyiv', 42)         // → "→ Kyiv 42 km"   ({0}, {1} are the arguments)
tn('raion_red', 3)              // → plural: "3 raions red" / "3 райони червоні"
  • t(key, ...args) — a string, with {0}, {1} placeholders. Falls back to English if a key is missing in the current language.
  • tn(key, n) — plural forms. English and French use key_1 / key_n; Ukrainian additionally uses key_2 (2–4) and key_5 (5+, and 11–14), following the real rule.
  • oblName(uid) / oblFull(uid), raionName(r), townName(n), riverName(n), poiName(n), bridgeName(n) — geographic names per language.
  • pn(place) — a place name that came from a post: kept in Ukrainian for the Ukrainian UI, transliterated otherwise.

Static markup is translated by attribute, so HTML stays readable:

<button data-t="m_obl">My oblast…</button>        <!-- textContent -->
<p data-t-html="d_p1"></p>                        <!-- innerHTML, for strings with <b> -->
<button data-t-title="t_center">◎</button>        <!-- title attribute -->
<input data-t-ph="h_ph">                          <!-- placeholder -->

Choosing the language#

Detected from the browser on first open (uk/ru → Ukrainian, fr → French, anything else → English), then changeable from the flags in the disclaimer (🇬🇧 EN · 🇺🇦 UA · 🇫🇷 FR) or from Language in the More panel (⋯). The choice is stored per device in localStorage and the page reloads.

Post text is not UI text#

Posts are shown in the original Ukrainian in Ukrainian mode. In English and French they are translated by translate.py: the offline glossary at once, then a machine translation (DeepL or Google Cloud, with a key) replaces it in the background, only for a language somebody is reading. French has no offline glossary: without a machine translator it shows the English. On the Light page a tapped row shows the original under the translation. Details: TECHNICAL.md §8.

Adding a string#

  1. Add the key to all three blocks in static/i18n.js — en, uk, fr.
  2. Use it: t('my_key') in JavaScript, or data-t="my_key" in markup.
  3. CI fails if a key exists in en but is missing from uk or fr.

Adding a language#

Add a block to I18N and an entry to LANGS, LFLAG and LCODE, add the language's column to OBL_N, RAION_N and the other name tables (the index comes from LI()), and set LOCALE(). Nothing else in the page needs to change.

Appendix C · The regression corpus

The regression corpus

Everything this app draws comes from patterns matched against free text written by people in a hurry, in two languages, with declensions. That produces a steady trickle of misreads. In one evening: знищено on a warehouse read as a target shot down, колом swallowing a town 700 km away, a government press release closing a live Shahed track, a stated north of the oblast dropped on the oblast centre.

Every one of those was found by a person looking at the map. That is a detection method that does not work at three in the morning during a mass attack, and it does not scale past one pair of eyes.

A case is a real post plus the parse it must produce. replay re-parses all of them and reports what changed, so a fix that quietly breaks another reading fails the build instead of appearing on somebody's phone during a raid.

The three states#

state meaning enforced
pending captured; nobody has said whether the recorded parse is right no — reported as drift only
verified a human confirmed this is the correct reading yes — any difference fails
known_bad a human confirmed this reading is wrong, and it is not fixed yet in reverse — when it stops reproducing, the suite says so

pending exists so that capturing a bug cannot freeze it as the expected answer. known_bad exists so a fix gets noticed and promoted instead of forgotten.

Even a pending case earns its keep: the press release that closed a live track showed up as drift in two channels before anyone had labelled it.

Where cases actually come from#

Not a terminal. The person who notices a wrong marker is holding a phone, looking at the map, during a raid — and a capture step that needs a command line is a capture step that never happens.

So the primary path is the ⚑ button on every marker. Tap it, pick what is wrong (wrong place, wrong threat type, not a threat at all, already gone, something else), optionally add a line, send. It changes nothing on the map and says so — a button that silently moved markers would be a way to lie to everyone else looking at the same screen. It files the post for review.

Flags land on /admin under Flagged readings, and become corpus cases with:

python scripts/corpus.py flags            # read them
python scripts/corpus.py flags --import   # turn them into pending cases, mark them handled

Nothing identifying is stored: the post, the reading, the reason, the optional note. No address, no location. The rate limit (40 a day) uses the same throwaway daily hash as the usage counter, which is regenerated every midnight and cannot be reversed.

That also means the people using this map — the ones who know the ground far better than any parser does — are the ones finding the misreads, instead of one person checking a map alone.

Reviewing: on /admin, not in a terminal#

The 260-odd pending cases are reviewed from the browser, under Review readings on /admin. One post, what this build makes of it, and three answers: correct, wrong (with a note), skip. It works on a phone.

The corpus file ships inside the deployed image and cannot be written from a machine in Amsterdam, so the verdicts are stored on the volume and come home with:

python scripts/corpus.py pull      # merge the verdicts into data/corpus.jsonl
python scripts/corpus.py replay

review in the terminal still exists and does the same thing; use whichever is in front of you.

Day to day#

python scripts/corpus.py harvest            # add real posts the app has already collected, as pending
python scripts/corpus.py review             # walk the pending ones: y / n / s / q
python scripts/corpus.py replay             # the regression run  (also runs inside pytest)
python scripts/corpus.py stats

When something looks wrong on the live map, capture it while you are looking at it:

python scripts/corpus.py add --channel kyiv_airdef --text "Склад … знищено" \
    --note "this is damage on the ground, not a shoot-down"
python scripts/corpus.py review             # mark it known_bad, then fix it, then re-review

That turns "I saw something odd on the map" into a case that can never come back silently.

What is compared#

project() in scripts/corpus.py is the contract. It keeps, per marker: status, type, place, oblast, coordinates (2 dp), heading, count, jet, likely, altitude, position confidence and quadrant — plus the post's feed tags and whether it was considered relevant.

It deliberately leaves out the human-readable evidence sentences. Those get reworded for clarity often, and a corpus that fails on wording is a corpus somebody switches off within a week. Confidence is in: high on a guess is a safety bug, not a cosmetic one. Tags are in: the MiG-31K banner and the one-second ballistic cadence are driven by tags, not by markers, and both have had bugs.

Reviewing well#

The question is never "did the app do something reasonable". It is "is this what the post actually says":

  • a place named, a type not named → unspecified threat, never a drone glyph;
  • a destruction word about something on the ground → damage, never a shoot-down;
  • policy, money or programme vocabulary → no marker at all;
  • a confidence of high only when the post named a town the gazetteer knows.

When in doubt, mark it known_bad with a note rather than verified. A wrong expectation is worse than no expectation: it makes the suite defend the bug.
